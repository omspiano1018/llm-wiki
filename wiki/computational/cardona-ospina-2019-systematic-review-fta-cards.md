## Summary
This 2019 PRISMA systematic review from Cardona-Ospina et al. (Universidad Tecnologica de Pereira, Colombia) evaluates FTA cards as a tool for viral RNA preservation and transport in fieldwork, synthesizing 47 studies. The review confirms that FTA cards are effective for most RNA virus preservation and downstream detection (all 47 studies achieved viral RNA amplification), identifies three extraction methods in use, and raises a key biosafety concern: only 14/47 studies tested virus inactivation, with one documented case of persistent infectivity. The review recommends treating dried blood spots as potentially infectious.

## Key Contributions
- First PRISMA-compliant systematic review of FTA cards for viral RNA
- 47 studies confirmed: FTA cards enable successful RT-PCR amplification of viral RNA from animal and human samples
- Three distinct RNA extraction methods documented across literature (no standardized protocol)
- 95.7% of studies extracted total RNA before targeted amplification
- Biosafety gap: 13/14 inactivation studies confirmed virus non-infectivity; 1 study found residual infectivity — dried blood spots potentially infectious
- High variability in storage conditions across studies; maximum temperature and duration not definitively established
- Animal viruses (poultry, wild birds, suids, bovids) account for 38.3% of publications

## Methodology and Architecture
PRISMA-compliant search: "FTA cards" AND "RNA" in PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science (June 2018); 175 records retrieved → 47 included after screening.

FTA card mechanism: cellulose matrix impregnated with chemical denaturants and free radical scavenger; cells lysed on contact; DNA/RNA bound to paper; bacteria inactivated; proteins denatured; samples dried at room temperature.

Quality scoring (10-item custom checklist): source, storage time, temperature, amplicon size, extraction method, purification, cDNA synthesis, PCR conditions, quantification (RT-qPCR), sequencing confirmation.

Success criterion: targeted RNA fragment amplified from cDNA by RT-PCR, or RNA enabling NGS.

## Results
- 100% success rate for viral RNA amplification across 47 studies (though protocols varied substantially)
- RNA virus types covered: avian influenza, swine viruses, bovine viruses, and others in agricultural, wildlife, and human health contexts
- Storage conditions varied: room temperature to refrigerator; days to years; results generally positive but systematic long-term comparisons lacking
- Inactivation: largely untested; one documented case of residual virus infectivity creates biosafety concern for laboratory workers
- FTA cards remain attractive for fieldwork in LMICs or regions without cold chain infrastructure
