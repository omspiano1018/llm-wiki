## Summary
This 2026 University of Connecticut review by Zhen et al. is the most comprehensive comparison of drying technologies for nucleic acid-LNP systems, covering mRNA alongside siRNA, saRNA, circRNA, pDNA, miRNA, and the CRISPR system. The paper reviews conventional lyophilization and emerging alternatives — continuous freeze-drying via spin-freezing, thin film freeze-drying, spray freeze-drying, spray drying, and alcohol dilution freeze-drying — and emphasizes how the specific structure of each nucleic acid modality affects drying challenges. Historical context spans from fomivirsen (first FDA NA drug, 1998) through Onpattro (2018) and COVID-19 vaccines (2020) to the 2023 Nobel Prize for nucleoside modification.

## Key Contributions
- First systematic comparison of drying strategies across all major NA-LNP modalities; shows that nucleic acid structure (single/double-stranded, circular/linear, size) fundamentally determines drying behavior and optimal excipients
- mRNA-LNP bleb structure (solvent-filled vesicular compartment with mRNA inside) confirmed by multiple independent groups and shown to be affected by buffer, lipid composition, preparation method, and lyophilization process
- Onpattro (siRNA-LNP) as stable liquid at 2-8°C for 3 years is the benchmark that mRNA-LNP lyophilization aims to approach
- Novel alcohol dilution freeze-drying proposed to mitigate freeze-concentration effects that destabilize LNPs during conventional lyophilization
- mRNA hydrolysis accelerated by: temperature, humidity, dielectric constant of solvent, low ionic strength, highly acidic/alkaline pH, longer mRNA length
- 22 nucleic acid therapeutics approved by FDA/EMA as of 2026

## Methodology and Architecture
Drying methods compared:
| Method | Key features | Challenges |
|---|---|---|
| Standard lyophilization | Freeze → sublimation → desorption | Slow, scalability, bleb effects |
| Spin-freezing lyophilization | Continuous; thin shell on vial wall | Heterogeneous heat transfer |
| Thin film freeze-drying | Thin film for faster heat transfer | Surface area requirements |
| Spray freeze-drying | Atomized droplets frozen in cryogen | Shear during atomization |
| Spray drying | Hot gas drying | Heat/shear damage to LNPs |
| Alcohol dilution | Avoids aqueous freeze-concentration | Novel; limited validation data |

NA-LNP structural considerations: siRNA (short double-stranded, 21 nt), mRNA (long single-stranded, 1000+ nt), saRNA (self-amplifying; larger), circRNA (covalently closed; exonuclease-resistant), pDNA (large circular double-stranded), CRISPR (RNP or RNA+protein complex).

## Results
- mRNA-LNPs require −60°C to −80°C (Comirnaty) or −15°C to −50°C (Spikevax) storage; lyophilization goal is room temperature or refrigerator stability for months
- Bleb structures in mRNA-LNPs observed independently by Brader, Sebastiani, Chen, Li, Bi, Nourafkan, and others
- SiRNA-LNP (Onpattro): chemically modified (2'-F/2'-OMe); liquid formulation stable 3 years at 2-8°C — structural stability advantage vs. mRNA
- Alternative drying methods promising but not yet validated at manufacturing scale for mRNA-LNPs
- Spray drying generates dry powder suitable for inhalation/nasal routes; important for mucosal vaccine delivery
