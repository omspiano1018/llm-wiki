## Summary
This 2023 review from Xian et al. (University of Queensland) focuses specifically on how nanobiotechnology strategies — including molecular mRNA modifications, self-assembly into nanostructures, and nanoparticle encapsulation — address mRNA instability. The review is organized around destabilization mechanisms (in-line hydrolysis, oxidation, RNase degradation, decapping, deadenylation) and corresponding protective strategies. Unlike delivery-focused reviews, the emphasis is on structural protection and shelf-life enhancement.

## Key Contributions
- In-line hydrolysis (2'-OH nucleophilic attack on phosphate) is the primary spontaneous mRNA degradation mechanism; accelerated by temperature, alkaline pH, and RNases
- Oxidative degradation (guanosine → 8-oxoguanosine) triggers no-go decay via ribosome stalling
- Chemical modifications: N1-methyl-pseudouridine (m1Ψ), 2'-OMe, 2'-F protect against RNase cleavage and reduce immunogenicity
- LNP encapsulation via electrostatic/pi-pi stacking interactions limits water access and enzyme degradation
- Lyophilization with trehalose/sucrose cryoprotectants preserves mRNA-LNP integrity and potency
- Self-complementary mRNA sequences forming double-stranded regions resist single-strand-specific RNases

## Methodology and Architecture
Review organized in four sections:
1. **Destabilization factors**: in-line hydrolysis, ROS oxidation, cellular decapping/deadenylation/miRNA-mediated decay
2. **Molecular strategies**: 5' cap optimization, 3' poly(A) tail design, UTR engineering, nucleotide substitution (m1Ψ), codon optimization
3. **Nano-encapsulation**: LNPs (ionizable lipid + helper lipid + PEG-lipid + cholesterol), polymer nanoparticles (PLGA, PEI-based)
4. **Manufacturing considerations**: freezing/thawing protocols, lyophilization with cryoprotectants

Storage benchmarks: Pfizer-BioNTech −80°C/9 mo; 4°C/~2.5 mo; Moderna −20°C/6 mo; 4°C/1 mo.

## Results
- mRNA stability directly correlates with final protein expression (intact mRNA → translation)
- LNP encapsulation not only aids delivery but measurably improves stability against enzymatic degradation and long-term storage degradation
- Lyophilization extends mRNA-LNP shelf life significantly when appropriate cryoprotectants are used
- Nanostructured mRNA assemblies and polymer encapsulation provide alternatives or complements to LNP-based protection
- Balance between stability and translatability must be considered: over-structured mRNA can impair ribosome loading
