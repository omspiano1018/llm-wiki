## Summary
This 2025 McGill University review by Khan et al. provides a comprehensive overview of lyophilization (freeze-drying) as a strategy to overcome the thermostability limitations of mRNA-LNP vaccines. The paper covers LNP structure and instability, the three-step lyophilization process, formulation components (cryoprotectants, buffers, surfactants), process parameters, storage conditions, and the existing regulatory gap for lyophilized mRNA-LNP products. The context is that both Comirnaty (−80°C) and SpikeVax (−20°C) have very limited room-temperature stability (6 h and 12 h, respectively), creating severe distribution challenges especially in LMICs.

## Key Contributions
- Lyophilization converts mRNA-LNPs to stable dry powder via: freezing → primary drying (sublimation) → secondary drying (desorption)
- Water removal prevents hydrolysis and reduces molecular mobility, slowing all degradation reactions
- Cryoprotectants (sucrose, trehalose, mannitol) form glassy matrices that immobilize LNPs, preventing aggregation and fusion during drying
- LNP bleb structure (solvent-filled compartment with mRNA) influences lyophilization behavior and must be considered in process design
- Regulatory gap identified: no FDA standards specific to lyophilized mRNA-LNP; critical quality attributes (particle size, PDI, EE, mRNA integrity, reconstitution) proposed as regulatory framework
- mRNA-LNP applications extend beyond COVID-19 to multiple cancers and infectious diseases (Zika, HIV, influenza, RSV, CMV, VZV, rabies)

## Methodology and Architecture
LNP components and their roles in stability:
- **Ionizable lipid**: encapsulates mRNA at low pH; neutral at physiological pH; facilitates endosomal escape
- **PEG-lipid**: regulates particle size, reduces aggregation via steric barrier
- **Phospholipid (e.g., DSPC)**: high transition temperature; lamellar phase stabilizes LNP membrane
- **Cholesterol**: fills lipid gaps, enhances membrane rigidity and integrity

mRNA instability: 2'-OH groups → spontaneous cleavage under heat, alkaline pH, metal ions; also susceptible to oxidation, depurination, deamination, and mRNA-lipid adduct formation.

Lyophilization framework from viral vector vaccines (liposomal delivery systems) transferred as starting point for process development.

## Results
- Comirnaty: 6 h RT stability; SpikeVax: 12 h RT stability — lyophilization target is days to months at 2-8°C or room temperature
- Sucrose and trehalose are the most widely studied cryoprotectants for mRNA-LNP lyophilization; optimal concentration important to prevent crystallization and maintain glassy state
- LNP structural rearrangement during lyophilization (bleb formation influenced by buffer, lipid, and process) must be controlled
- Parallel with viral vector vaccines (e.g., live-attenuated vaccines commonly lyophilized) provides existing experience base
- Multiple promising lyophilization protocols published; none yet at commercial scale for mRNA-LNPs
