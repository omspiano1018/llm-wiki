## Summary
This 2026 critical perspective from Veríssimo, Freire, and Pereira reviews the use of ionic liquids (ILs) and deep eutectic solvents (DESs) as multifunctional platforms for stabilizing and delivering nucleic acid biopharmaceuticals. Unlike prior descriptive reviews, this work emphasizes physicochemical mechanisms, structure-property relationships, and translational challenges. It covers siRNA, mRNA, and ASO formulations and includes a SWOT analysis of IL/DES technology for pharmaceutical applications. The nucleic acid therapeutics market is ~USD 6.94 billion (2025), projected to reach USD 12.24 billion by 2029.

## Key Contributions
- Mechanistic analysis of how IL/DES tunable ionic environments and hydrogen-bond networks preserve RNA/DNA structure against hydrolysis, oxidation, and nuclease activity
- Cholinium- and ammonium-based ILs demonstrated to stabilize duplex DNA, triplexes, and G-quadruplexes better than conventional aqueous buffers
- IL/DES can function as API-ILs — active pharmaceutical ingredients that simultaneously stabilize and deliver nucleic acids
- Surface-active ionic liquids (SAILs) integrate molecular protection and delivery in a single molecular entity
- DESs offer analogous stabilization roles to ILs with additional formulation flexibility via hydrogen-bond donor/acceptor tuning
- SWOT framework identifies cytotoxicity and regulatory gaps as key barriers to clinical translation

## Methodology and Architecture
The paper synthesizes a decade of research on ILs/DESs in nucleic acid applications, structured around:
- **Stabilization mechanisms**: ionic shielding of negatively charged RNA backbone, hydrogen-bond network formation reducing water activity, reduction of RNase accessibility
- **Delivery mechanisms**: cholinium ILs modulate membrane permeability enabling transdermal and intracellular transport (Tanner et al. 2018; Mitragotri 2024)
- **Key IL classes**: cholinium-based (most biocompatible), imidazolium-based, ammonium-based
- **Key DES classes**: cholinium chloride/urea, choline/glycerol, natural DESs (NADESs)
- Comparison with conventional LNP-based delivery systems; ILs/DESs proposed as complementary or alternative approaches

## Results
- Biocompatible, hydrated ILs preserve RNA conformation over extended periods under conditions that degrade RNA in standard buffers
- Cholinium geranate (CAGE) ionic liquid enables transdermal siRNA delivery without cytotoxicity (Zakrewsky et al. 2014; Mitragotri 2024)
- DES formulations of cholinium chloride-based systems stabilize mRNA at elevated temperatures
- IL-robed RNA constructs demonstrate protection against RNase degradation while retaining cellular uptake capacity
- Market and clinical pipeline data indicate accelerating adoption of nucleic acid therapeutics, increasing urgency for improved stabilization solutions
