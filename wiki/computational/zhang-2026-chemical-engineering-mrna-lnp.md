## Summary
This 2026 National University of Singapore review by Zhang et al. provides a systematic framework linking chemical engineering strategies to mRNA-LNP stability outcomes. It covers intrinsic mRNA sequence and structural determinants of stability, chemical modifications (nucleotide substitutions, cap analogs, poly(A) engineering, reversible 2'-OH acylation), advanced RNA architectures (circular RNA, self-amplifying RNA), LNP formulation engineering (low-immunogenicity and thermostable LNPs), and storage/transport solutions. The review addresses clinical translation challenges and provides forward-looking perspectives on emerging mRNA technologies.

## Key Contributions
- GC-rich sequences form stronger (triple H-bond) base pairs, but excessive GC can impede translation and recruit decay machinery
- Exoribonucleases (XRN1, exosome complex) and endoribonucleases (AGO, SMG6) as primary enzymatic degradation agents; PABPC1 protects poly(A) tail
- Reversible 2'-OH acylation: novel chemical protection of primary hydrolysis site; conceptually removes water attack point during storage
- circRNA: covalently closed; no 5'/3' termini for exonuclease attack; sustained expression potential
- saRNA: encodes viral RdRp for self-amplification; enables 10-100x dose reduction with same or better efficacy
- Thermostable LNPs: higher Tm lipids, novel ionizable lipids; extended refrigerator stability over standard formulations
- N1-methylpseudouridine (m1Ψ) remains central to mRNA stability and immunogenicity management

## Methodology and Architecture
Stability determinant hierarchy:
1. **Intrinsic sequence features**: GC content, codon usage (tRNA availability), stem-loops, hairpins, AU-rich elements
2. **RNase activity**: exoribonucleases (5'→3': XRN1 after decapping; 3'→5': exosome after deadenylation) and endoribonucleases (AGO/SMG6 guided by miRNA/siRNA); NMD and NSD quality control pathways
3. **Physicochemical factors**: ionic fluctuations, oxidative conditions (ROS), pH changes, temperature

Chemical engineering strategies reviewed:
- 5' cap: m7G standard; ARCA (anti-reverse cap analog) improves orientation and translation; CleanCap technology
- Poly(A) tail: ~120 nt optimal; highly extended tails reduce translation efficiency
- UTR optimization: sequences protecting against miRNA targeting and NMD
- Codon optimization: aligning with abundant tRNA pools to reduce ribosome stalling
- Nucleotide modification: m1Ψ, 2'-OMe, 2'-F — RNase resistance and immune evasion
- Reversible 2'-OH acylation: protects phosphodiester cleavage site during storage

## Results
- m1Ψ-modified mRNA: reduced immunogenicity, improved translational yield; central to COVID-19 vaccine success (Karikó/Weissman, Nobel 2023)
- Circular RNA: growing production and delivery system evidence base; covalent closure eliminates 5'/3' exonuclease vulnerability
- saRNA systems: successfully demonstrated dose sparing in preclinical vaccine studies; replicon RNA platform advancing clinically
- Thermostable LNPs: several candidate formulations demonstrated extended stability at 2-8°C (weeks to months vs. days for standard formulations)
- Lyophilization and spray drying: effective for extending shelf life; spray drying particularly promising for inhalable/mucosal vaccine routes
