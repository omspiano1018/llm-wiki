## Summary
Martin and Ephrussi (2009) review the mechanisms of mRNA localization to subcellular compartments, establishing that spatial regulation of gene expression is far more prevalent than previously appreciated (71% of Drosophila embryo transcripts are spatially localized). The review covers cis-acting localization elements (zipcodes) in 3'UTRs, trans-acting RNA-binding proteins, RNA transport granule assembly, and motor-driven transport along cytoskeletal elements, with detailed examples from yeast (ASH1), Drosophila (bicoid, oskar), and neurons (beta-actin, CamKIIa).

## Key Contributions
- 71% of >3000 Drosophila embryo transcripts show spatially distinct localization patterns (Lecuyer et al., 2007); hundreds of mRNAs localize in mammalian neurons and migrating fibroblasts
- Four advantages of mRNA localization: (1) spatial restriction of protein production; (2) temporal resolution via local stimuli; (3) economy — multiple protein copies from single localized mRNA; (4) protection of other compartments from toxic proteins
- Cis-acting localization elements (zipcodes): most commonly in 3'UTR; range 5–6 to several hundred nt; often redundant; form stem-loop secondary structures; structure more important than sequence
- Three localization mechanisms: (1) active directed transport along cytoskeleton (most common); (2) local stabilization/regulated degradation; (3) diffusion-and-trapping
- bicoid mRNA 3'UTR: BLE1 (50 nt stem loop) for nurse cell-to-oocyte transport; additional stem loops for localization steps; requires dimerization via hairpin loops for Staufen binding
- ZBP1/IMP1: binds beta-actin mRNA zipcode; represses translation during transport; Src-mediated tyrosine phosphorylation releases ZBP1 enabling local translation at lamellipodia
- RNA transport granules: contain localized mRNAs + RNA-binding proteins + molecular motors + translational repressors; related to stress granules and P-bodies

## Methodology and Architecture
- Review integrating Drosophila genetics, yeast genetics, mammalian cell biology (FISH, live imaging), and bioinformatics
- Classic model systems: ASH1 mRNA (yeast bud tip); bicoid/oskar/nanos (Drosophila embryo poles); VegT/Vg1 (Xenopus vegetal pole); beta-actin (fibroblast lamellipodia); MBP (oligodendrocyte processes); CamKIIa and beta-actin (neuronal dendrites/growth cones)
- Nuclear history matters: pre-mRNA processing in nucleus recruits RNA-binding proteins that determine cytoplasmic localization
- Motor proteins: kinesin (plus-end, microtubule), dynein (minus-end, microtubule), myosin V (actin cables; ASH1 in yeast via She2/She3/Myo4)

## Results
- bicoid localization elements: BLE1 50-nt stem loop required for oocyte entry; mutations preserving stem-loop structure but changing sequence still permit localization; RNA dimerizes via hairpin loops; Staufen binding requires dimerization
- ZBP1 tyrosine phosphorylation by Src kinase: in response to local signaling, releases ZBP1 from beta-actin mRNA → local translation in lamellipodia → directed cell motility
- FMRP (Fragile X mental retardation protein): represses translation of dendritic mRNAs including CamKIIa; mGluR signaling activates FMRP-regulated translation for synaptic plasticity
- CPEB: regulates cytoplasmic polyadenylation of oskar mRNA in Drosophila oocytes; controls timing of local translation
- She2/She3/Myo4 (yeast ASH1): She2 binds ASH1 zipcode in nucleus; She3 adaptor bridges to myosin V Myo4; active transport along actin cables to bud tip
