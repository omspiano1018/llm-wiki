## Summary
Cech (2009) challenges the canonical view that catalysis evolved linearly from ribozymes to RNP enzymes to protein enzymes. Using comparative analysis of diverse RNP enzymes (RNase P, Group I/II introns, spliceosome, ribosome, SRP, telomerase), he argues that RNP enzymes are permanent fixtures of contemporary biology — not transitional intermediates — and that in some cases the RNA component has elaborated new functions rather than being replaced by protein.

## Key Contributions
- Challenges linear ribozyme → RNP → protein enzyme evolutionary model
- RNase P: bacterial RNA is a ribozyme; eukaryotic RNA requires multiple protein partners; human mitochondrial RNase P is entirely protein but assembled from pre-existing proteins (new pathway, not evolution from RNP)
- Group I introns: self-splicing ribozymes in vitro; require protein helpers in vivo (CYT-18 tyrosyl-tRNA synthetase stabilizes Neurospora intron structure)
- Group II introns: RNP with encoded reverse transcriptase; proposed evolutionary ancestor of spliceosomal snRNAs
- Ribosome: peptidyl transferase center is RNA-catalyzed; protein components fill structural cracks and improve efficiency
- SRP: RNA and SRP54/Ffh GTPase conserved across all three domains of life; RNA enables conformational flexibility and ribosome binding
- Telomerase: RNA template component maintained with no evidence of replacement by protein; RNA component elaborated new functions in some organisms

## Methodology and Architecture
- Comparative essay using biochemical, structural, and phylogenetic data from published literature
- Evidence from two sources: cross-domain comparison of functionally equivalent RNPs, and biochemical dissection of RNA vs. protein contributions within each RNP
- Special RNA properties justifying retention: ability to span large distances, conformational flexibility at internal loops/bulges, RNA-RNA interactions with ribosome
- RNA world hypothesis: self-replicating RNA as solution to chicken-and-egg origin problem; remains attractive but unproven

## Results
- RNase P RNA: present in common ancestor of all life; bacterial RNA active alone in vitro; eukaryotic RNA cannot act without protein partners; human mitochondrial RNase P is three unrelated proteins — independent evolutionary solution to tRNA maturation
- Group II intron structure homologous to domain V of spliceosomal U6/U2 snRNAs; mechanistic similarities establish them as analogous systems; common ancestry vs. convergent evolution cannot be definitively resolved
- Ribosome: phylogenetically conserved rRNA core; multiple glycine/lysine/arginine-rich ribosomal protein extensions penetrate deep into rRNA; rRNA expansion segments added in eukaryotic nuclear rRNAs; mitochondrial rRNAs show structural simplification
- Telomerase RNA: defines the sequence added to telomeres; acts as template without genetic drift; no evolutionary trend toward RNA replacement
- CYT-18 crystal structure reveals how a protein enzyme has acquired a secondary function stabilizing a ribozyme fold
