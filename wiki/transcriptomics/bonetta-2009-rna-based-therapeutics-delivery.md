## Summary
A 2009 Cell Analysis piece by Laura Bonetta surveying the state of RNA-based therapeutics entering clinical trials. As of 2009, only two RNA drugs had FDA approval: fomivirsen (antisense, 1998) and pegaptanib (RNA aptamer). The article catalogs the next wave—siRNAs, shRNAs, antagomirs—and identifies **tissue-specific delivery** as the field's central bottleneck. Key trials at the time included Alnylam's inhaled RSV siRNA, Opko's bevasiranib for AMD, and Isis's mipomersen for hypercholesterolemia.

## Key Contributions
- Snapshot of the RNA therapeutic pipeline at a pivotal moment (pre-mRNA vaccine era).
- Documents that delivery efficiency, not potency, is the limiting factor for all RNA modalities.
- Identifies emerging miRNA antagonist strategy: antagomirs against miR-21 (cardiac fibrosis) and miR-122 (HCV replication).
- Shows viral vector-delivered shRNA + RNA decoy combination for HIV (City of Hope / Benitec).

## Methodology and Architecture
Analysis article based on interviews with industry and academic scientists. RNA therapeutic classes discussed:

| Class | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Antisense ASO | RNase H-mediated or steric block | Mipomersen (apoB-100) |
| siRNA | RISC-mediated mRNA cleavage | Bevasiranib (VEGF) |
| shRNA | Dicer-processed hairpin → siRNA | HIV shRNA + RNA decoy |
| Aptamer | 3D structure-mediated protein binding | Pegaptanib (VEGF) |
| Antagomir | miRNA sequestration | Anti-miR-21, anti-miR-122 |

Key delivery vehicles: lipids, polymers, viral vectors. Off-target effects controlled by chemical modifications.

## Results
- Bevasiranib phase II AMD trial completed, but confounded by TLR3 immunostimulation (non-specific anti-angiogenic effect).
- Mipomersen phase II/III trials showed LDL lowering in hypercholesterolemia.
- Alnylam ALN-RSV01 (inhaled siRNA) entered phase II for RSV lung infection.
- ASOs still unable to cross blood-brain barrier or penetrate heart/muscle.
