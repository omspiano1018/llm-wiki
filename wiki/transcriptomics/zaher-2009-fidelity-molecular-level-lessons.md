## Summary
Zaher and Green (2009) review the mechanistic basis of translational fidelity in bacteria, synthesizing decades of biochemical and structural studies. The central theme is that the ribosome uses induced-fit conformational changes — triggered by cognate codon-anticodon interaction — to coordinate information transfer across its two subunits (~70 Å apart), enabling kinetic proofreading at both the tRNA selection and translation termination steps. Defects in translational fidelity have severe biological consequences including neurodegeneration, while controlled infidelity through prion-like mechanisms can be adaptively beneficial.

## Key Contributions
- Fidelity comparison: DNA replication 1/10^8–10^10; transcription ~1/10^4; translation ~1/10^3–10^4; stop codon recognition by release factors ~1/10^5
- Kinetic proofreading: small energetic differences between cognate/near-cognate substrates are amplified by multiple discrimination steps using GTP hydrolysis energy (Hopfield 1974; Ninio 1975)
- Induced-fit mechanism: cognate codon-anticodon base pairing triggers flipping of 16S rRNA nucleotides A1492/A1493 out of helix 44 to contact minor groove of codon-anticodon duplex; G530 repositions; 30S domain closure; signals transmitted to EF-Tu GTPase via interdomain communication
- Two rounds of discrimination: initial selection (codon recognition, pre-GTP hydrolysis) and proofreading (post-GTP hydrolysis, pre-peptidyl transfer)
- Translation termination: RF1 (UAA/UAG) and RF2 (UAA/UGA) recognize stop codons via protein-RNA interactions; conserved GGQ tripeptide catalyzes peptidyl-tRNA hydrolysis; accuracy ~1/10^5
- Biological consequences of infidelity: defective aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase editing → neurodegeneration (Lee et al., 2006); [PSI+] prion (eRF3 conformation) enables adaptive stop codon readthrough

## Methodology and Architecture
- Review focused on bacterial (E. coli) translation; notes that eukaryotic release factors (eRF1, eRF3) are not evolutionarily related to bacterial RF1/RF2/RF3
- Ribosome architecture: 30S subunit (decoding center) + 50S subunit (peptidyl transferase center); three tRNA binding sites (A, P, E)
- Elongation cycle: ternary complex delivery → GTPase activation → accommodation → peptidyl transfer → EF-G-mediated translocation
- Comparison with DNA polymerases: polymerases use geometric calipers measuring Watson-Crick pair geometry; ribosomes use induced-fit + kinetic proofreading since decoding and polymerization centers are separated

## Results
- A1492/A1493 flip mechanism: cognate codon-anticodon causes these adenosines to flip out and probe minor groove geometry; near-cognate substrates do not trigger full conformational change; this is the structural basis of initial selection
- 30S domain closure: rotation of the shoulder domain of the 30S subunit transmits cognate recognition signal to EF-Tu GTPase ~70 Å away
- Proofreading: after GTP hydrolysis, near-cognate aa-tRNAs have elevated rejection rate before accommodation into A/A site; ratio of proofreading to initial selection contributes multiplicatively to overall accuracy
- Neurodegeneration from editing defects: alanyl-tRNA synthetase editing domain mutations in mice cause misincorporation of serine for alanine → protein misfolding → unfolded protein response → apoptosis in neurons
- [PSI+] adaptive benefit: readthrough of stop codons reveals hidden phenotypic variation that can be selected under stress conditions; epigenetically heritable through prion propagation
