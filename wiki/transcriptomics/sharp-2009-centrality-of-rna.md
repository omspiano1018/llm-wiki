## Summary
This 2009 Cell essay by Phillip A. Sharp (co-discoverer of mRNA splicing) offers a historical and prospective synthesis of RNA's roles in biology. Sharp argues that RNA's unique ability to simultaneously encode genetic information, form complex secondary and tertiary structures, catalyze reactions, and regulate gene expression places it at the center of both ancient life (RNA world) and modern cellular regulation. The essay traces the conceptual journey from the central dogma through the discovery of split genes, the RNA world, RNAi, and miRNAs, and concludes that the traditional transcription factor model of gene regulation is progressively insufficient to account for the pervasive role of RNA in regulating its own processing, transport, translation, and decay.

## Key Contributions
- **RNA world and ribosome as RNA machine**: RNA catalysis (ribozymes) and the conclusion that peptidyl transferase activity resides in rRNA support the view that RNA preceded proteins as the primary cellular catalyst.
- **Split genes and alternative splicing**: mRNA splicing (discovered 1977) revealed that >90% of human transcription units are alternatively spliced; the combinatorial explosion of isoforms redefines the concept of a "gene."
- **RNA as a regulatory hub**: Each processing step (splicing, capping, polyadenylation) is coupled to gene expression through RNA-protein interactions; the EJC links splicing history to nuclear export and NMD.
- **Small RNA revolution**: RNAi (Fire et al., 1998) and miRNAs (Lee et al., 1993) established that small RNAs silence genes post-transcriptionally and/or at the chromatin level; miRNAs are estimated to regulate hundreds to thousands of human genes.
- **Deep sequencing as a transformative tool**: CLIP-seq and RNA-seq (then emerging) are revealing the full scope of RNA-protein interactions and RNA isoforms, driving new discoveries about RNA biology.
- **iPS cells and transcription factors**: Reprogramming of somatic cells to pluripotency by four transcription factors (Takahashi & Yamanaka, 2006) re-validates the transcription factor model even as RNA-level regulation becomes more appreciated.

## Methodology and Architecture
Essay (no original data). Draws on published literature to argue conceptual points:

- Evolutionary logic: RNA's multi-functionality (information storage, catalysis, regulatory base-pairing) is unique among biomolecules and explains its evolutionary persistence.
- Gene regulatory landscape: transcription factor networks interact with RNA processing, chromatin modification, and small RNA pathways in an integrated, multi-layered system.
- Emerging technologies: CLIP-seq (Licatalosi et al., 2008, demonstrating NOVA splicing networks in brain) and deep-sequencing-based RNA-seq as key drivers of discovery.

## Results
- >90% of human transcription units are alternatively spliced (Wang et al., 2008); the number of distinct protein isoforms far exceeds the ~23,000 protein-coding gene count.
- The ribosome is an RNA machine: crystallographic studies showed that the peptidyl transferase center is composed entirely of rRNA, with no protein side chains close to the reacting atoms.
- miRNA conservation: many human miRNA seed sequences are conserved from vertebrates to insects (Drosophila) and nematodes (C. elegans), indicating ancient regulatory origin.
- EJC: deposited at every exon-exon junction; provides a biochemical "record" of splicing history used in the cytoplasm for NMD surveillance and translational regulation.
- CLIP-seq of NOVA proteins revealed hundreds of alternatively spliced targets in brain, suggesting most neuronal proteins have brain-specific isoforms regulated by RBPs.
