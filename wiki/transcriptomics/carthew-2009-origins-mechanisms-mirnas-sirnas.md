## Summary
Carthew and Sontheimer (2009) provide a unified mechanistic review of siRNA and miRNA biogenesis and silencing across eukaryotes, covering the structure and catalytic mechanism of Dicer enzymes, Argonaute protein families, RISC assembly, and the divergence of these pathways across organisms from C. elegans to humans. The review traces the history of small RNA biology from the lin-4 discovery in 1993 to the recognition of RNA silencing as a fundamental regulatory mechanism affecting chromatin, transcription, and translation.

## Key Contributions
- Unified framework showing siRNAs and miRNAs share the same core machinery (Dicer + Argonaute) despite different precursors and targets
- Dicer mechanism: PAZ domain binds 2 nt 3' overhang of dsRNA end; substrate extends ~two helical turns to intramolecular RNase III dimer; both strands cleaved to yield ~21–23 nt duplex with 2 nt 3' overhangs and 5' monophosphates
- Organism-level diversity: single Dicer in mammals/nematodes; Drosophila Dcr-1 (miRNA) vs. Dcr-2 (siRNA); four DCL proteins in Arabidopsis
- Argonaute Ago-clade proteins bind si/miRNAs via PAZ domain; PIWI domain provides slicer activity (AGO2 in humans/Drosophila)
- miRNA biogenesis in animals: nuclear Drosha/DGCR8 microprocessor generates pre-miRNA → Exportin-5 export → cytoplasmic Dicer processing
- siRNA pathway: long dsRNA → Dicer → siRNA duplex → RISC assembly → target slicing at positions 10/11 from 5' end
- ATP role in Dicer: stimulates Drosophila Dcr-2 and C. elegans Dcr-1; dispensable for human Dcr; ATPase domain has autoinhibitory effect on hDcr

## Methodology and Architecture
- Narrative review integrating genetics, biochemistry, and structural studies in C. elegans, Drosophila, Arabidopsis, and mammals
- Historical timeline: lin-4 (1993) → RNAi discovery (1998) → silencing-associated ~20–25 nt RNAs in plants (1999) → siRNA characterization → miRNA as broad class (2001)
- Three small RNA categories: siRNAs, miRNAs, piRNAs (piRNAs reviewed separately by Malone/Hannon)
- RISC assembly: Dicer hands duplex to Argonaute; passenger strand discarded; guide strand programs target specificity
- miRNA silencing mechanisms: deadenylation, decapping, P-body sequestration, translational inhibition

## Results
- Dicer length ruler: PAZ-to-RNase III distance determines small RNA size; different Dicers produce slightly different-length products across organisms
- ATPase domain of hDcr has autoinhibitory effect on dsRNA processing efficiency; conditions for relief unknown
- Organisms with multiple Dicers show functional specialization (Drosophila: Dcr-1 for miRNA, Dcr-2 for siRNA)
- Both siRNA- and miRNA-programmed RISC can elicit target cleavage (slicing) if guide RNA has perfect complementarity
- miRNA targets with central bulges resist slicing; regulation occurs via translational inhibition and mRNA destabilization
