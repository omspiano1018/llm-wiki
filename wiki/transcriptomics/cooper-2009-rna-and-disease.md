## Summary
This 2009 Cell review by Cooper, Wan, and Dreyfuss examines how mutations that disrupt the RNA or protein components of ribonucleoprotein complexes (RNPs) cause human disease. The paper focuses especially on defects in pre-mRNA splicing—covering both cis-acting mutations within affected genes and trans-acting mutations in the splicing machinery—and on RNA gain-of-function diseases where expanded repeat RNAs sequester essential splicing regulators (myotonic dystrophy). The review includes a comprehensive table of >35 diseases caused by defects in diverse RNA-dependent functions, from ribosome biogenesis to telomerase to translation, and surveys RNA-based therapeutic strategies.

## Key Contributions
- **Alternative splicing as the predominant disease mechanism**: Deep sequencing data show >90% of human multi-exon genes produce multiple isoforms; this complexity is both the source of proteomic diversity and a major vulnerability to disease-causing mutations.
- **SMN and SMA**: SMN1 deletion causes insufficient full-length SMN protein; SMN2 carries a C→T mutation in exon 7 that creates an hnRNP A1 binding site and destroys an ESE, causing exon 7 skipping. SMA severity inversely correlates with SMN2 copy number.
- **RNA gain-of-function in DM1/DM2**: Expanded CUG repeats (DMPK, DM1) or CCUG repeats (ZNF9, DM2) form nuclear foci that sequester MBNL splicing regulators; persistent fetal splicing patterns in adult tissues (cardiac troponin T, insulin receptor, chloride channel) cause multisystemic disease.
- **Spliceosome mutations and retinitis pigmentosa**: Mutations in core snRNP proteins (PRPF31, PRPF8, HPRP3) specifically affect retinal photoreceptors despite ubiquitous spliceosome expression—unexplained tissue specificity.
- **RNA therapeutics**: Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) can redirect splicing by blocking aberrant splice sites (IKBKAP in familial dysautonomia) or promoting exon inclusion (SMN2 exon 7 in SMA); early animal model proof-of-concept available by 2009.
- **Comprehensive disease table** (Table 1): Lists >35 diseases caused by RNA/RNP dysfunction spanning all RNA classes and biological processes.

## Methodology and Architecture
Review integrates genetic, biochemical, and cell biology data across multiple disease models:

- **Splicing mechanism**: Spliceosome (U1, U2, U4/U6, U5 snRNPs + >100 proteins); Sm core assembly by SMN complex (ATP-dependent assemblyosome); minor spliceosome (U11/U12/U4atac/U6atac) handles AT-AC introns.
- **Splicing code**: Degenerate consensus splice sites (5'GU...AG3', branch point) supplemented by ESEs (recognized by SR proteins) and ESSs (recognized by hnRNPs); combinatorial code determines exon inclusion.
- **MBNL sequestration model**: CUG/CCUG repeat RNA hairpins bind MBNL proteins; reduced free MBNL shifts splicing toward fetal patterns; complementary upregulation of CUGBP1 (by PKC-mediated phosphorylation) further drives mis-splicing.
- **Therapeutic approaches**: ASO chemistry (2'-OMe, phosphorothioate, morpholino); redirecting Sm core assembly to restore SMN2 function; trans-splicing (spliceosome-mediated trans-splicing, SMaRT) to correct mutations at the RNA level.

## Results
- Titin (316 exons), ryanodine receptor 1 (106 exons), and dystrophin (79 exons) represent extreme examples of multi-exon genes where even single splice-site mutations cause severe disease.
- SMN2-based therapeutic strategy (ASO blocking ISS-N1 intronic silencer to promote exon 7 inclusion) restores SMN levels in cell culture and mouse models, leading to the clinical development of nusinersen (approved 2016).
- DM1 mouse models: expressing expanded CUG repeats reproduces many features of the disease; MBNL1 knockout mice phenocopy the splicing defects.
- TDP-43 (TARDBP) mutations found in ALS/FTLD; TDP-43 normally represses nonconserved cryptic exon inclusion; disease mutations or cytoplasmic mislocalization leads to widespread cryptic exon incorporation and aberrant mRNA isoforms.
