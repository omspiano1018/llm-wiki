## Summary
This 2009 Cell review by Moore and Proudfoot synthesizes two decades of evidence showing that pre-mRNA processing—5' capping, splicing, and 3' cleavage/polyadenylation—is not a series of post-transcriptional events but is physically and mechanistically coupled to RNA Pol II transcription and to downstream mRNA fate decisions (nuclear export, translation, decay). The central organizing principle is the Pol II carboxyl-terminal domain (CTD), whose phosphorylation state changes as Pol II progresses through the transcription cycle, providing a dynamic platform for sequential recruitment of processing factors. The paper also highlights gene loops, nuclear pore associations, and the exon junction complex (EJC) as key physical connections linking nuclear and cytoplasmic gene expression.

## Key Contributions
- **CTD as master coordinator**: CTD Ser5P (early elongation) recruits capping enzymes; CTD Ser2P (late elongation) recruits polyadenylation factors. This order enforces the sequence of processing events.
- **Poised Pol II and bidirectional transcription**: Many inducible genes have pre-engaged Pol II at their promoters producing short abortive transcripts in both sense and antisense directions; P-TEFb/Cdk9 switches this to productive elongation.
- **Gene loop model**: Promoter and terminator of active genes come into close proximity (detected by 3C); polyadenylation complex components (Ssu72, Pta1) interact with TFIID/TFIIB at the promoter, connecting 3' processing to transcription initiation.
- **Cotranscriptional splicing**: Splicing factors are recruited to the elongating Pol II; nucleosome positioning influences splicing through "kinetic coupling"—slower Pol II over weak splice sites increases exon inclusion probability.
- **EJC and mRNA fate**: Splicing deposits the EJC ~24 nt upstream of each exon junction; EJC marks mRNA for nuclear export and serves as a downstream-intron signal for NMD surveillance during the pioneer round of translation.
- **Nuclear pore association and transcriptional memory**: In yeast, activated inducible genes relocate to the NPC for direct RNA injection into the cytoplasm; short-term repressed genes remain NPC-associated for faster reactivation.

## Methodology and Architecture
The review integrates structural studies of Pol II (cramer 2004), phospho-CTD biochemistry, genome-wide Pol II ChIP data, 3C chromatin looping assays, and single-gene biochemical/genetic experiments in both yeast and mammalian systems. Key conceptual frameworks:

- Pol II CTD: 26 (yeast) or 52 (mammals) heptad repeats; Ser5P by TFIIH/Cdk7 at initiation; Ser2P by P-TEFb/Cdk9 during elongation.
- 5' capping: triphosphatase + guanyl transferase (fused in mammals; separate Ceg1/Cet1 in yeast) + methyltransferase act co-transcriptionally on the first ~25 nt of the emerging transcript.
- 3' polyadenylation: cleavage/polyadenylation complex (CPF/CPSF) interacts with CTD Ser2P via CID domains; TFIID/TFIIB interact with polyA complex components at the promoter.
- EJC core: eIF4AIII, Mago/Y14, MLN51 deposited ~24 nt upstream of every splice junction by the spliceosome.

## Results
- Genome-wide studies showed promoter-proximal paused Pol II as widespread, especially at developmental/inducible genes in Drosophila and mammals.
- HIV-1/Tat paradigm: the short 5' TAR hairpin plus Tat recruits P-TEFb, demonstrating that an RNA element can serve as the trigger for productive elongation.
- 7SK RNA + Hexim forms an inhibitory complex sequestering P-TEFb in an inactive pool; provides a global translational brake on Pol II elongation.
- Gene loop formation requires specific polyA complex components (Ssu72, Pta1 in yeast); mutations disrupt both efficient termination and promoter-associated reinitiation.
- EJC downstream of a premature termination codon (PTC) triggers NMD; ~30% of alternatively spliced isoforms are estimated to be NMD substrates, making NMD a major regulator of the transcriptome.
