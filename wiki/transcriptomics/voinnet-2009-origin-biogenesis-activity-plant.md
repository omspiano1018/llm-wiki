## Summary
Voinnet (2009) provides a comprehensive review of plant microRNA (miRNA) biology in Arabidopsis thaliana, describing the evolutionary origins of MIR genes, the multi-step nuclear biogenesis pathway (DDL → DCL1/HYL1/SE/CBC complex in D-bodies → HEN1 methylation → AGO1 loading), and the predominant silencing mechanism of target mRNA slicing via near-perfect base complementarity. The review highlights key differences from metazoan miRNA biology and emphasizes the blurring boundary between miRNA and siRNA classes in plants.

## Key Contributions
- MIR gene evolution: young MIR genes arise from inverted duplications (proto-MIR phase processed as siRNAs by DCL3/DCL4) and progressively evolve into DCL1-processed single miRNA genes through concerted target co-evolution; alternatively, MIR genes evolve spontaneously from random inverted repeats
- Biogenesis pathway: Pol II transcribes pri-miRNA → DDL stabilizes → D-body processing by DCL1/HYL1/SE/CBC complex → export by HASTY → HEN1 methylation protects from SDN nucleases → guide strand loaded into AGO1 for slicing
- Plant miRNAs use near-perfect complementarity to single coding-region target sites (vs. metazoan imperfect complementarity to multiple 3'UTR sites)
- Predominant mechanism is slicing (AGO1 slicer activity), though translational inhibition also occurs for some miRNAs
- Plant sRNA landscape dominated by 24 nt heterochromatin-associated siRNAs (DCL3/AGO4/RdDM); miRNAs are the second most abundant class
- AGO1 and AGO4 have demonstrated slicer activity among ten Arabidopsis AGO family members

## Methodology and Architecture
- Narrative review of genetic and biochemical studies primarily in Arabidopsis thaliana
- Small RNA classes distinguished by size (DCL1: 18–21 nt; DCL2: 22 nt; DCL3: 24 nt; DCL4: 21 nt), Dicer, and AGO proteins
- Three consensus RNA-silencing steps: dsRNA processing, 3'-O-methylation by HEN1, RISC loading
- Target regulation mechanisms: (1) RNA endonucleolytic cleavage (slicing); (2) translational repression; (3) DNA/histone methylation

## Results
- DCL1-deficient and AGO1-deficient mutants show severe developmental defects, establishing miRNA essentiality
- miRNA-deficient mutants fail to acquire appropriate cell identities during embryonic and post-embryonic growth
- Single-cell organisms (Chlamydomonas reinhardtii) also produce miRNAs, refuting the multicellularization-origin hypothesis
- HEN1 methylation: 2'-O-methyl group on sRNA 3' end blocks SDN-class exonuclease degradation
- MIR gene evolutionary model: inverted duplication → proto-MIR (siRNA-like, processed by DCL3/4) → young MIR (DCL4-processed, ta-siRNA-like) → ancient MIR (DCL1-processed, single miRNA)
