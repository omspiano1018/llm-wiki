## Summary
This 2024 review from Kornienko et al. systematically examines the factors governing RNA stability, covering both internal molecular features (primary structure, 2'-OH reactivity, poly(A) tail length, RNA secondary/tertiary structure, RNA–protein interactions) and external environmental conditions (temperature, pH, ionic composition, oxidative stress). The review consolidates mechanisms of RNA degradation and translates them into practical storage and stabilization recommendations, particularly relevant for mRNA therapeutics and biobank management.

## Key Contributions
- Comprehensive mechanistic account of RNA hydrolysis via 2'-OH nucleophilic attack on phosphodiester bonds, forming a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate intermediate
- RNA phosphodiester bonds are 200 times less stable than DNA under physiological conditions (Lindahl et al.)
- Alkaline pH (8.0+) and elevated temperature are the dominant accelerants of RNA degradation
- Poly(A) tail length and modification (m6A, hydroxymethylation) modulate stability and protein binding
- Chemical modifications (2'-OMe, 2'-F, pseudouridine) substantially increase RNA resistance to nucleases
- Formulation of practical recommendations: use RNase-free environments, avoid divalent metal ions, store at low temperature or in dry/desiccated form

## Methodology and Architecture
The review synthesizes published biochemical and biophysical studies on RNA stability mechanisms. Key structural elements analyzed include:
- **Primary structure**: nucleotide composition, GC content, codon usage
- **Secondary structure**: stem-loops and hairpins protecting against nuclease access
- **Tertiary structure**: G-quadruplexes and pseudoknots providing additional stability
- **Poly(A) tail**: short (<50 nt) vulnerable; long (>150 nt) may impair ribosome binding
- **RNA-binding proteins**: protect specific regions from degradation

Environmental factors reviewed: temperature (rate-limiting for hydrolysis), pH (alkaline = faster hydrolysis), ionic composition (divalent cations Ca2+, Mg2+ catalyze cleavage), oxidative stress (ROS, ozone attack on bases and backbone).

## Results
- At pH 7.4 and 37°C, intramolecular transesterification is the primary spontaneous degradation route
- N6-methyladenosine (m6A) in poly(A) tail promotes binding to poly(A)-binding proteins, potentially affecting stability
- Pseudouridine (Ψ) and N1-methyl-pseudouridine (used in Pfizer/Moderna COVID vaccines) reduce innate immune stimulation and improve in vivo stability
- Desiccation strongly inhibits RNA degradation; atmospheric water restores reactivity even in solid state
- Storage at −80°C remains the gold standard; commercial matrices (RNAstable, GenTegra) claim multi-year room-temperature stability
