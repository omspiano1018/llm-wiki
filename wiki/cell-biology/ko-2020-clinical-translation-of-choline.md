## Summary
The first end-to-end clinical translation of CAGE: a kilogram-scale CAGE1:2 gel (CGB400, 40% w/w) was taken through seven translational steps — scale-up, characterisation, stability, mechanism of action, dose determination, GLP toxicology in minipigs, and a Phase 1b human study. In a 12-week open-label trial in 26 patients with mild-moderate facial rosacea, CGB400 produced a marked reduction in inflammatory lesions, establishing the first ionic liquid-based dermatological clinical product.

## Key Contributions
- First report of a DES/ionic liquid completing all seven translational steps to human clinical use
- Kilogram-scale synthesis via one-step salt metathesis with no critical scale-up issues
- Identifies unique characterisation challenges for DES (no new covalent bond; NMR and HPLC insufficient alone)
- GLP toxicology in minipigs preceded human study
- Phase 1b (n=26): marked reduction of inflammatory rosacea lesions over 12 weeks

## Methodology and Architecture
- **CGB400**: CAGE1:2 + propylene glycol + d-limonene + hydroxypropyl cellulose gel
- **Scale-up**: commercial reactor; batch >3 kg; quality control by water content, pH (~8.5), conductivity (~1.3 mS/cm)
- **Characterisation**: 1H NMR, HPLC, MDSC (Tg ≈ −68°C), TGA, FTIR — multiple orthogonal methods required
- **Mechanism**: stratum corneum lipid extraction → deep skin penetration → pathogen neutralisation + TLR2 modulation + KLK5 inhibition
- **Preservative-free**: CAGE1:2 inherent antimicrobial activity eliminates need for parabens

## Results
- Scale-up: successful at >3 kg without formulation changes
- Phase 1b (12 weeks, n=26): marked reduction in number of inflammatory lesions
- No safety signals in GLP minipig toxicology
- First ionic liquid/DES to complete clinical translation in dermatology
