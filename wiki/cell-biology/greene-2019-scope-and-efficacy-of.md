## Summary
CAGE eradicates biofilms of 11 clinically isolated ESKAPE pathogens — including MDR strains — at concentrations as low as 3.56 mM (0.156% v:v) within 2 hours. At 0.1% v:v, a >3 log10 reduction in S. aureus biofilm viability occurs within 15 minutes. Molecular modelling reveals that geranate ion and geranic acid integrate into bacterial membranes, causing thinning and disruption of membrane homeostasis. These properties position CAGE as a superior broad-spectrum topical antiseptic compared to current FDA-recognised antiseptic ingredients.

## Key Contributions
- First systematic antibiofilm characterisation of CAGE across 11 ESKAPE pathogens (24 h and 72 h biofilms)
- Minimum biofilm eradication concentration (MBEC): 3.56 mM — lower than most standard antiseptics
- EPS layer disruption within 120 min enables penetration to underlying cells
- Molecular mechanism: membrane integration → thinning → homeostasis disruption

## Methodology and Architecture
- **Synthesis**: choline bicarbonate + geranic acid salt metathesis; purity by 1H/13C NMR; conductivity 43 mS/cm
- **Biofilm model**: MBEC inoculators (Innovotech); 24 h and 72 h cultures on polystyrene pegs
- **Pathogens**: 11 ESKAPE clinical isolates including MDR strains
- **Neutralisation**: serial ethanol wash before viability counts (essential to stop residual CAGE activity)
- **Molecular modelling**: MD simulation of geranate/geranic acid with bacterial membrane bilayer

## Results
- MBEC: 3.56 mM in 2 h across ESKAPE panel
- 0.1% v:v: >3 log10 S. aureus biofilm reduction in 15 min (upon contact)
- 0.1% v:v: EPS disruption in 120 min
- Efficacy consistent across 24 h and 72 h biofilms and MDR strains
- Outperforms standard-of-care topical antiseptics in potency and speed of action
