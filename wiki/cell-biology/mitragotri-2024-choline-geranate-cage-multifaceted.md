## Summary
A 10th-anniversary review of CAGE (Choline And GEranic acid), the first drug delivery ionic liquid to enter human clinical trials. Synthesised from choline bicarbonate and geranic acid via salt metathesis, CAGE sits at the ionic liquid / deep eutectic solvent (DES) boundary. Since its 2014 discovery it has been applied to transdermal, oral, buccal, sustained-release, antiseptic, and tissue-ablation drug delivery. As of 2024, CAGE products have been tested in >200 patients across multiple Phase 1 and Phase 2 studies, including a successful Phase 2 trial in Atopic Dermatitis.

## Key Contributions
- Historical perspective: CAGE discovery (2014) → first clinical trial → commercial product (10-year arc)
- Mechanism: penetrates stratum corneum, extracts/fluidises skin lipids; [Chol]1[Ger]2 stoichiometry most potent for permeation
- Macromolecule delivery: dextrans up to 150 kDa and siRNA delivered transdermally
- Imiquimod-CAGE achieves >10-fold greater topical delivery than Aldara™
- CAGE became the first drug delivery IL included in a commercially launched product

## Methodology and Architecture
- **Synthesis**: salt metathesis of choline bicarbonate + geranic acid; stoichiometry variants [Chol]1[Ger]1/2/4
- **Skin penetration**: fluidises stratum corneum lipids; Hansen solubility parameters of ILs match skin lipids better than conventional enhancers
- **Macro-delivery**: 2D NMR shows choline–geranate association promotes co-penetration into stratum corneum
- **Oral route**: CAGE-insulin (10 U/kg) → 45% blood glucose reduction in rats

## Results
- >200 patients in Phase 1/2 clinical studies
- Phase 2 Atopic Dermatitis: positive outcome
- Transdermal: insulin, siRNA, dextrans to 150 kDa, bacteriophage delivered in vivo or ex vivo
- CAGE outperforms conventional chemical permeation enhancers across Hansen solubility parameter analysis
