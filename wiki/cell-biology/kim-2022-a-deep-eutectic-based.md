## Summary
SEAPORT (Self-Emulsifying APO-Releasing Therapeutic) is a CAGE1:2/NMP/water ternary formulation of apomorphine (APO) for Parkinson's disease. Upon subcutaneous injection, NMP diffuses away and CAGE1:2 self-emulsifies in water, entrapping APO and dramatically slowing its release. APO plasma concentration remained detectable for 48 h in pigs (vs. ~2 h for the clinical comparator Apokyn), projecting a reduction from 3 injections/day to ~3 injections/week in humans.

## Key Contributions
- First CAGE-based sustained-release subcutaneous depot system
- Novel self-emulsification mechanism driven by differential CAGE1:2/NMP/water miscibility
- APO solubility tripled to 30 mg/mL vs. 10 mg/mL in Apokyn
- 48-h detectable APO plasma concentration in pigs — a remarkable improvement over Apokyn
- All non-CAGE excipients already FDA-listed in approved subcutaneous products (facilitates regulatory path)

## Methodology and Architecture
- **Formulation (SEAPORT)**: CAGE1:2 10% v/v + NMP 42.7% v/v + water 47.3% v/v + APO 30 mg/mL + SMB (antioxidant) + PEG 3350 (stabiliser)
- **Self-emulsification**: NMP diffuses away post-injection → CAGE1:2 disperses in water as microemulsion → APO entrapped
- **Stability**: LC-MS confirmed >90% APO retained over 7 days at 4, 25, 37°C
- **In vivo**: PK in rats (extended vs. Apokyn); PK in pigs (48 h detection); PK simulation for human dosing projection
- **Therapeutic window**: APO MEC 4 ng/mL, MTC 10 ng/mL — tight window requiring sustained, controlled release

## Results
- APO solubility: 30 mg/mL in SEAPORT (3× Apokyn)
- Stability: >90% APO over 7 days at all temperatures tested
- Pigs: 48-h detectable plasma APO (vs. ~2 h Apokyn)
- Projected human dosing: 3 injections/week vs. 3 injections/day (current standard)
- Ex vivo: self-emulsification confirmed in rat skin and gel models
