## One-line Summary
Review of room temperature biospecimen storage modalities for anatomic pathology and biorepositories, comparing FFPE, chemical matrices, stabilization solutions, lyophilization, and formalin-free fixatives.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Systematic comparison of four room-temperature biospecimen storage modalities: (1) dry chemical/polymer matrices, (2) stabilization solutions, (3) lyophilization/freeze-drying, (4) formalin-free fixatives
- Identifies limitations of formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue for nucleic acid analyses
- Discusses FTA cards as the best-studied chemical matrix for DNA preservation
- Reviews commercial products including Biomatrica (DNAstable, RNAstable, SampleMatrix), IntegenX (GenTegra RNA), and QIAGEN AllProtect
- Motivates development of robust room-temperature storage to reduce frozen storage costs

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Literature review of PubMed and vendor information
- Evaluation criteria: nucleic acid quality, storage duration, compatibility with downstream analyses (PCR, sequencing, microarrays)
- FFPE issues: cross-linking, fragmentation of nucleic acids (~1 mutation/500 bases), loss of enzymatic activity
- FTA paper (Flinders Technology Associates): cellulose matrix with uric acid, detergent, chelating agent; cells lysed, DNA bound; no RNA storage recommended by manufacturer
- Chemical matrices (Biomatrica DNAstable/RNAstable): proprietary compositions minimizing oxidation, hydrolysis, and nuclease activity; samples dried and stored
- AllProtect (QIAGEN): liquid stabilization solution preserving DNA, RNA, and protein in fresh tissue for up to 7 days at RT or 1 year refrigerated
- Lyophilization: preserves histomorphology, DNA, RNA, and proteins; RNA shows moderate degradation after 1-2 years

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- FTA paper: utility demonstrated for >7 years by forensic/evolutionary studies; DNA from 60 surgical specimens stored 49 days at RT performed equally to fresh DNA for PCR and IgH gene rearrangement
- Biomatrica DNAstable: buccal/blood/semen DNA stored 45 months (~3.66 years) showed limited degradation vs. −20°C controls by real-time PCR
- Biomatrica SampleMatrix: genomic DNA stored 1 year = −20°C controls (qPCR, gel, multiplex STR)
- Biomatrica/IntegenX RNA: 336 RNA samples stored 2 weeks and shipped at RT = −80°C controls for RNA quality and real-time PCR
- Commercial chemical matrix vendors claim real-time stability >2 years; accelerated aging suggests 12 years for RNA, 60 years for DNA
- AllProtect: tissue preserved 7 days at RT, DNA for 6 months; composition proprietary
- Lyophilized tissue: histomorphology preserved; RNA shows moderate degradation after 1-2 years

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Independent long-term data lacking for most commercial matrices; most studies are vendor-sponsored
- No peer-reviewed validation of chemical matrices for whole exome/genome sequencing or cDNA microarrays
- FTA paper: manufacturer does not recommend RNA storage; formal whole-exome/genome studies absent
- Formalin-free fixatives show improved nucleic acid preservation but face validation costs and barrier to replacing FFPE
- No current room-temperature technology preserves DNA, RNA, and protein simultaneously in tissue for years
- Lyophilization approaches vary and RNA degradation occurs after 1-2 years

## 7. Glossary
- **FFPE**: Formalin-Fixed Paraffin-Embedded; standard clinical tissue preservation; limits nucleic acid quality
- **FTA card**: Filter paper treated with chemical mixture that lyses cells and preserves DNA at room temperature
- **Lyophilization**: Freeze-drying; removes water by sublimation; preserves biological materials
- **DNAstable / RNAstable**: Biomatrica commercial products for room-temperature nucleic acid storage in dry form
- **AllProtect**: QIAGEN stabilization solution for fresh tissue; preserves DNA, RNA, protein
- **Anhydrobiosis**: Natural phenomenon in extremophiles (tardigrades, brine shrimp) where desiccation enables long-term survival
- **IgH gene rearrangement**: Immunoglobulin heavy chain gene rearrangement test used to assess DNA quality
