## One-line Summary
The foundational report introducing **Good's buffer ionic liquids (GB-ILs)**: 20 self-buffering, biocompatible ILs made by neutralizing five Good's buffers (Tricine, TES, CHES, HEPES, MES) with four organic cations ([Emim], [N1111], [N2222], [N4444]); they buffer in the physiological range, stabilize BSA secondary structure better than conventional ILs, are non-toxic to *Vibrio fischeri*, and form aqueous biphasic systems (ABS) extracting BSA at 100% in a single step.

## 2. Key Contributions
- **First synthesis of Good's buffer ionic liquids (GB-ILs)** using Good's buffers as the IL **anions** — a route to ILs that intrinsically buffer near physiological pH, removing the need to add a separate (potentially metal-chelating) buffer to a protein solution.
- Built a **20-member library**: five Good's buffer anions (Tricine, TES, CHES, HEPES, MES) × four cations (1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium [Emim], tetramethyl- [N1111], tetraethyl- [N2222], tetrabutylammonium [N4444]) via a simple green neutralization in water.
- Showed GB-ILs **buffer in water with the same mid-point pH and ranges as the parent Good's buffers** (and generally higher buffer capacity), and formulated **universal buffers** (MES+HEPES+CHES) with a near-linear profile over pH 5.2–10.4.
- GB-ILs give **greater BSA secondary-structure (α-helix) stabilization** than the corresponding conventional bromide ILs; molecular docking + QSAR (log K(HSA)) localize ion binding sites and rank cation affinity.
- GB-ILs are **non-toxic to *Vibrio fischeri*** (Microtox), with structure–toxicity trends for cation core, alkyl chain length, and anion identity.
- GB-ILs **form ABS** with inorganic/organic salts and extract BSA with **100% single-step efficiency**.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

### Rationale
- Hydrophilic ILs dissociate in water to give weakly basic solutions; simply adding a buffer fails because IL acidity/basicity swamps the buffer effect. Prior buffer-like ILs used non-inert anions unsuitable for biochemistry.
- Good's buffers are zwitterionic amino-acid (N-substituted taurine/glycine) derivatives that satisfy Good's criteria (chemically inert, non-toxic, no metal binding, pKa 6–8). Being zwitterions, they can serve as IL anions → **self-buffering protein-stabilizing ILs**.

### Synthesis & characterization
- **Neutralization**: hydroxide forms ([Emim][OH], [N1111][OH], [N2222][OH], [N4444][OH]) added drop-wise to excess equimolar Good's buffer; stirred 12 h, evaporated, washed (MeCN:MeOH 1:1), filtered, vacuum-dried 3 days.
- Water content <0.05 wt% (Karl–Fischer); structures confirmed by ¹H/¹³C NMR; melting points by DSC.
- **Potentiometric titration** (0.05 M GB-IL vs 0.05 M NaOH/HCl, 20 °C) → pH profiles and buffer capacity.
- **Thermal stability of BSA** via DLS hydrodynamic radius (R_H) vs temperature 25–75 °C → denaturation temperature T_d.
- **IR (amide I/II) + Gaussian curve fitting** → BSA secondary-structure fractions.
- **Molecular docking** (AutoDock Vina, BSA PDB 3v03; DFT B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p) charges) → ion binding sites & free energies.
- **QSAR log K(HSA)** (COSMO-RS, TURBOMOLE) → predicted serum-albumin binding affinities.
- **Microtox** (*Vibrio fischeri*, 30 min) → EC₅₀.
- **ABS**: ternary phase diagrams with Na₂SO₄ or K₃C₆H₅O₇; BSA extraction efficiency (EE_BSA%).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Physical state**: imidazolium GB-ILs and several ammonium GB-ILs are room-temperature liquids/low-melting solids; a few ammonium salts melt >100 °C but remain useful as aqueous media.
- **Self-buffering**: mid-point pH and buffering ranges of GB-ILs in water ≈ those of the parent Good's buffers; buffer capacities generally **higher** than the free GBs (except Tricine-ILs). Universal GB-IL buffer ([N1111][MES]+[N1111][HEPES]+[N1111][CHES]) linear over **pH 5.2–10.4**.
- **BSA thermal stability**: T_d of BSA in 0.05 M Tricine = 56 °C; conventional bromides 48–56 °C ([N4444]Br aggregates BSA, T_d 48 °C, dropping to 40 °C at 0.5 M). GB-ILs behave like conventional ILs on T_d **but require no added buffer**.
- **Secondary structure**: free BSA α-helix = 57.6%. Helicity in GB-ILs is **higher than in conventional ILs and increases with GB-IL concentration**; α-helix order [N4444][Tricine] > [Emim][Tricine] > [N2222][Tricine] > [N1111][Tricine]. [N4444][Tricine] promotes aggregation via hydrophobic surface contact rather than unfolding.
- **Docking/QSAR**: cation–BSA binding free energies −2.5 ([N1111]), −4.0 ([N2222]), −4.9 ([N4444]), −4.1 ([Emim]) kcal mol⁻¹; affinity rises with alkyl chain length. log K(HSA) shows GB-ILs bind serum albumin more strongly than the bromide ILs. Stabilization attributed to **direct ion–protein binding**, not water-structure (Hofmeister) effects.
- **Toxicity (Microtox EC₅₀, higher = safer)**: GB-ILs generally non-toxic (EC₅₀ from 179.77 mg dm⁻³ for [N4444][CHES] up to 44 302.87 mg dm⁻³ for [N2222][MES]); most exceed the EU non-toxic threshold (EC₅₀ > 100 mg dm⁻³). Trends: ammonium < imidazolium toxicity; longer alkyl chain ([N4444]) more toxic ("side-chain effect"); anion order [MES] < [TES] < [HEPES] < [Tricine] < [CHES]. GBs are generally slightly less toxic than their GB-ILs.
- **ABS + extraction**: [N4444][GB] + Na₂SO₄ or K₃C₆H₅O₇ form ABS (IL-rich top phase); less-hydrophilic anions (CHES, MES) salt out more easily; Na₂SO₄ is a stronger salting-out agent than citrate. BSA extracted with **100% efficiency in a single step** ([N4444][Tricine]/[TES]/[HEPES] + K₃C₆H₅O₇), no protein detected in the salt phase.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Uses **imidazolium and tetraalkylammonium** cations; the most extraction-active ([N4444]) is also the most toxic — motivating the later switch to the more biocompatible **cholinium** cation ([[taha-2015-novel-biocompatible-and-self-buffering-ionic]]).
- ABS extraction demonstrated only for a model protein (BSA); real biomolecule mixtures, recovery/recycling, and downstream IL removal not characterized.
- Protein-focused; nucleic-acid stabilization not addressed (extended later by the same group → [[pedro-2018-cholinium-based-goods-buffers]]).

## 6. Related Work
- **Direct successor**: cholinium GB-ILs for IgY extraction ([[taha-2015-novel-biocompatible-and-self-buffering-ionic]]) — swaps imidazolium/ammonium for biocompatible cholinium and uses a biodegradable polymer (PPG 400) instead of salt for the ABS.
- **RNA extension**: cholinium GB-ILs for recombinant small-RNA stabilization ([[pedro-2018-cholinium-based-goods-buffers]]).
- Broader biocompatible-IL nucleic-acid landscape ([[verissimo-2026-ionic-liquids-deep-eutectic]]); choline–amino-acid ILs for mRNA delivery ([[yu-2026-ionic-liquids-as-alternative]]).

## 7. Glossary
- **GB-IL (Good's Buffer Ionic Liquid)**: IL using a Good's buffer as the anion; intrinsically self-buffering.
- **Good's buffers**: zwitterionic N-substituted taurine/glycine buffers (MES, TES, HEPES, Tricine, CHES) designed by Good et al. (1966) to be inert, non-toxic, non-metal-binding, with pKa 6–8.
- **Cations used**: [Emim] = 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium; [N1111]/[N2222]/[N4444] = tetramethyl-/tetraethyl-/tetrabutylammonium.
- **ABS (Aqueous Biphasic System)**: two immiscible water-rich phases (IL + salt here) for mild liquid–liquid extraction.
- **T_d**: thermal denaturation temperature of a protein, here tracked by DLS hydrodynamic radius.
- **Microtox / EC₅₀**: *Vibrio fischeri* bioluminescence-inhibition toxicity assay; higher EC₅₀ = lower toxicity.
- **log K(HSA)**: QSAR-predicted human-serum-albumin binding constant (COSMO-RS).
- **Hofmeister series**: ranking of ions by their effect on protein solubility/stability via water structure (here shown to be secondary to direct ion–protein binding).
