## One-line Summary
Comprehensive review of bacterial regulatory RNAs—riboswitches, protein-binding sRNAs, trans-acting base-pairing sRNAs, and CRISPR RNAs—covering their mechanisms, discovery, physiological roles, and remaining questions.

## 1. Document Information
- **Title**: Regulatory RNAs in Bacteria

- **Year**: 2009
- **Journal**: Cell 136, 615–628, February 20, 2009

## 2. Key Contributions
- Provides a unified taxonomy of bacterial RNA regulators: riboswitches, protein-binding sRNAs, base-pairing sRNAs, and CRISPR RNAs.
- Traces the history of bacterial sRNA discovery from RNA I (1981) and MicF (1984) through the 2001–2002 computational discovery boom.
- Describes the molecular mechanisms by which riboswitches sense metabolites and modulate transcription or translation via alternative RNA structures.
- Explains how trans-acting base-pairing sRNAs use the RNA chaperone Hfq to facilitate imperfect base-pairing with target mRNAs, modulating translation and stability.
- Introduces CRISPR RNAs as an acquired immune system protecting bacteria against phage and plasmids, predating the CRISPR-Cas revolution.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Riboswitches**: Bipartite structure—aptamer domain (ligand binding) plus expression platform (alternative hairpins affecting transcription termination or translation initiation). Metabolite binding shifts the equilibrium between conformers. Examples include FMN, thiamin pyrophosphate, SAM, lysine, guanine riboswitches in Bacillus subtilis and E. coli. Up to 2% of B. subtilis genes are riboswitch-regulated.
- **Protein-binding sRNAs**: Titrate global regulatory proteins away from their target mRNAs. CsrB/CsrC sequester CsrA (which binds GGA hairpins in mRNAs); 6S RNA sequesters σ70-RNA polymerase to modulate transcription of σ70-dependent genes; GlmY/GlmZ cascade controls glmS expression.
- **Base-pairing sRNAs**: Typically 50–300 nt transcripts from intergenic regions; use short imperfect complementarity to target mRNA 5'UTRs or ORFs; Hfq chaperone facilitates pairing and protects sRNAs from degradation. Mechanism: occlude ribosome-binding site (block translation) or cause coupled degradation of sRNA-mRNA duplex by RNase E.
- **CRISPR RNAs**: Clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats process into crRNAs containing spacer sequences homologous to phage/plasmid DNA; act via an unknown mechanism (in 2009) to inhibit DNA uptake or replication.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- In E. coli, ~80 small transcripts have been verified as of 2009, increasing the total gene count by ~2%.
- Systematic searches (computational, cloning-based, microarray) and deep sequencing have identified hundreds of candidate sRNA genes across diverse bacteria.
- Hfq-bound sRNAs often regulate stress responses: iron homeostasis (RyhB), outer membrane composition (MicF, MicC), quorum sensing (Qrr sRNAs in Vibrio), virulence (RNAIII in S. aureus).
- The glmS riboswitch is unique in acting as a ribozyme: it self-cleaves upon binding its cofactor glucosamine-6-phosphate, inactivating the mRNA.
- CRISPR spacer sequences match phage genomes, establishing CRISPR as an adaptive immune memory system.
- Some sRNAs (e.g., RNAIII in S. aureus, 514 nt) also encode small proteins, blurring the boundary between coding and noncoding RNA.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- The mechanism of CRISPR interference was unknown at time of publication.
- Many newly identified sRNAs lacked characterized targets or functions.
- The full extent of Hfq-independent sRNA regulation was unclear.
- How the stoichiometry of sRNAs and targets is balanced in cells was not well understood.
- Cross-species conservation of sRNAs (especially outside E. coli and S. aureus) remained underexplored.

## 7. Glossary
- **sRNA**: Small RNA; bacterial regulatory RNAs typically 50–500 nt.
- **Riboswitch**: Metabolite-sensing 5'UTR element that adopts alternative structures to control transcription or translation.
- **Aptamer domain**: The ligand-binding portion of a riboswitch.
- **Expression platform**: The downstream portion of a riboswitch that effects regulatory output (terminator/antiterminator or translation-blocking hairpin).
- **Hfq**: Bacterial RNA chaperone (homolog of Sm proteins); facilitates sRNA-mRNA base pairing and stabilizes sRNAs.
- **CRISPR**: Clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats; genomic array of phage/plasmid-derived sequences that generate crRNAs for adaptive defense.
- **CsrA**: Carbon storage regulator; an mRNA-binding protein titrated by CsrB/CsrC sRNAs.
- **6S RNA**: sRNA that sequesters σ70-RNA polymerase and modulates transcription of σ70-dependent promoters.
- **RNAIII**: 514 nt sRNA in S. aureus that also encodes delta-hemolysin; global regulator of virulence genes.
- **RNase E**: Major endoribonuclease in E. coli; degrades sRNA-mRNA duplexes and processes ribosomal RNA.
- **T-box**: Leader sequence in tRNA synthetase mRNAs that binds uncharged tRNAs to regulate expression.
- **glmS ribozyme**: Self-cleaving riboswitch that uses glucosamine-6-phosphate as cofactor.
