## One-line Summary
A review of the mechanisms and biological significance of mRNA localization, covering cis-acting localization elements (zipcodes), trans-acting RNA-binding proteins, RNA transport granule assembly, and motor-driven transport along cytoskeletal tracks.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Establishes that mRNA localization is far more prevalent than previously thought (71% of ~3000 Drosophila embryo transcripts show spatially distinct patterns)
- Catalogues the advantages of mRNA localization: spatial restriction, temporal resolution, economy of translation, protection from toxic proteins
- Systematically reviews cis-acting localization elements (zipcodes) in 3'UTRs (and some 5'UTRs/coding sequences), their redundancy, secondary structure requirements, and stepwise function
- Reviews trans-acting RNA-binding proteins (ZBP1/IMP1, Staufen, She proteins, FMRP, CPEB) and their roles in localization and translational regulation
- Describes RNA transport granule assembly, composition, and relationship to stress granules and P-bodies
- Reviews the molecular motors (kinesin, dynein, myosin V) and cytoskeletal elements that drive mRNA transport

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Narrative review integrating genetics (yeast, Drosophila), cell biology (FISH, live imaging), and bioinformatics
- Classic localized mRNAs: ASH1 (yeast bud tip); bicoid/oskar/nanos (Drosophila embryo poles); VegT/Vg1 (Xenopus vegetal pole); beta-actin (fibroblast lamellipodia, neuron growth cones); MBP (oligodendrocyte processes); CamKIIa (dendrites)
- Three localization mechanisms: (1) active directed transport (most common); (2) local stabilization/regulated degradation; (3) diffusion-and-trapping
- Localization elements: most often in 3'UTR; range from 5–6 to several hundred nt; often repeated/redundant; often form stem-loop structures; can function in multiple cell types
- Bicoid mRNA system detailed: 625 bp 3'UTR sufficient for anterior localization; BLE1 (50 nt stem loop) for nurse cell-to-oocyte transport; additional stem loops for anchoring; bicoid RNA dimerizes via hairpin loops; Staufen binding requires dimerization
- RNA granule assembly: RNPs → transport granules; related to stress granules and P-bodies; motor association via adaptor proteins

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Lecuyer et al. (2007): 71% of >3000 Drosophila embryo transcripts show spatially distinct localization by high-throughput in situ hybridization
- Mili et al. (2008): hundreds of mRNAs localize in migrating fibroblasts to specific subcellular compartments
- Martin and Zukin (2006): hundreds of mRNAs present in neuronal dendrites and synapses; local translation enables synaptic plasticity
- bicoid localization elements: BLE1 (50 nt stem loop) required for oocyte entry; separate elements for anchoring at anterior pole; mutations that preserve stem-loop structure but change sequence still permit localization (structure > sequence)
- ZBP1/IMP1: binds zipcode in beta-actin mRNA 3'UTR; required for lamellipodia localization; Src-mediated tyrosine phosphorylation releases ZBP1 and derepresses local translation
- FMRP: RNA-binding protein mutated in Fragile X syndrome; represses translation of dendritic mRNAs; associates with polyribosomes and RNA granules
- CPEB: regulates cytoplasmic polyadenylation of localized mRNAs in oocytes and neurons; activation releases translational repression
- She2/She3/Myo4 system in yeast: She2 recognizes ASH1 mRNA zipcode in nucleus; She3 bridges to myosin V Myo4; active transport to bud tip along actin cables

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- No universal consensus zipcode sequence has emerged; recognition appears to involve structure more than sequence
- How transport granules are disassembled and mRNAs released for translation at destinations is not well understood
- The mechanisms linking arrival at destination to translational derepression are incompletely characterized for most mRNAs
- The extent of mRNA localization in non-neuronal mammalian cell types is not fully characterized
- The relationship between RNA granules, stress granules, and P-bodies, and how mRNAs transition among them, needs clarification

## 7. Glossary
- **mRNA localization**: Targeting of specific mRNAs to distinct subcellular compartments for spatially restricted translation
- **Zipcode**: Cis-acting localization element in mRNA (most commonly in 3'UTR) that specifies the mRNA's subcellular destination
- **ZBP1/IMP1**: Zipcode-binding protein 1; RNA-binding protein that recognizes the zipcode in beta-actin mRNA and represses translation during transport
- **Staufen**: dsRNA-binding protein in Drosophila; required for bicoid mRNA anterior localization and oskar mRNA posterior localization
- **FMRP**: Fragile X mental retardation protein; RNA-binding protein that represses translation of dendritic mRNAs; mutated in Fragile X syndrome
- **CPEB**: Cytoplasmic polyadenylation element-binding protein; regulates translation of localized mRNAs via controlled poly(A) tail elongation
- **RNA transport granule**: Large mRNP complex containing localized mRNAs, RNA-binding proteins, motors, and other factors; represses translation during transport
- **P-body**: Cytoplasmic processing body containing mRNA decay and silencing machinery; related to RNA granules
- **Stress granule**: Cytoplasmic granule formed under stress; contains stalled translation initiation complexes
- **Kinesin**: Plus-end-directed microtubule motor; involved in transport of mRNAs to distal processes
- **Dynein**: Minus-end-directed microtubule motor; involved in retrograde mRNA transport
- **Myosin V**: Actin-based motor; involved in ASH1 mRNA transport in yeast (Myo4) and beta-actin mRNA in neurons
- **BLE**: Bicoid localization element; specific stem-loop structures within bicoid mRNA 3'UTR required for successive steps of localization
- **She2/She3/Myo4**: Yeast ASH1 mRNA localization complex; She2 (RNA-binding), She3 (adaptor), Myo4 (myosin V motor)
- **Synaptic plasticity**: Experience-dependent strengthening or weakening of synaptic connections; requires local translation of dendritically localized mRNAs
