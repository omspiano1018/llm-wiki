## One-line Summary
Synthesis and full thermophysical characterization of three biocompatible choline–amino acid ionic liquids (CAAILs) — cholinium alaninate [Ch][Ala], dicholinium glutamate [Ch]₂[Glut], and cholinium threoninate [Ch][Threo] — and their aqueous binary solutions, providing density, viscosity, conductivity, refractive index, excess-volume, and ionicity (Walden plot) reference data needed to design these "green" solvents for bioengineering applications.

## 2. Key Contributions
- **Synthesis of three CAAILs** ([Ch][Ala], [Ch]₂[Glut], [Ch][Threo]) at 313.15 K and 0.1 MPa via a base-then-cation neutralization route, validated by ¹H NMR and FTIR.
- **Comprehensive thermophysical characterization** of their aqueous binary solutions across 288.15–308.15 K: liquid density, refractive index, electrical conductivity, dynamic viscosity, torque, and shear stress.
- **Ionicity (degree of dissociation, α)** of each CAAIL determined as a function of composition using the **Walden plot** at 298.15 K — addressing an underexplored property for CAAILs.
- Density and excess-volume data correlated with established models (Connors–Wright; third-degree polynomials; Redlich–Kister); viscosity with VFTH (pure) and Arrhenius (solutions).
- Provides reference data underpinning CAAIL use as biocompatible solvents for biomolecule extraction/separation, where ionicity matters to avoid degradation.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

### Synthesis of CAAILs
Following the group's prior route: ethanolic amino acid (L-alanine, L-glutamic acid, or L-threonine) stirred 3 h at 313.15 K / 0.1 MPa with a slight stoichiometric excess of **KOH**; then **choline chloride** added and stirred ~12 h; vacuum filtration removes precipitated KCl; product dried by rotary evaporation (72 h, 323.15 K).
- **[Ch][Ala]** — cholinium alaninate (C₈H₂₀N₂O₃), purity >97% (¹H NMR)
- **[Ch][Threo]** — cholinium threoninate (C₉H₂₂N₂O₄), purity >98%
- **[Ch]₂[Glut]** — dicholinium glutamate (C₁₅H₃₅N₃O₆), purity >99% (2:1 cholinium:glutamate)

### Instrumentation
- Density: Anton Paar DSA-4500 M oscillating U-tube densimeter (±5×10⁻⁵ g·cm⁻³)
- Viscosity / torque / shear stress: Anton Paar MCR92 rheometer (cone–plate)
- Conductivity: Hanna EDGE EC meter
- Refractive index: ABBE 315 RS digital refractometer (589 nm)
- Water content: Mettler Toledo C20 Karl Fischer titrator
- Structure validation: PerkinElmer Spectrum Two FT-IR; Bruker ¹H NMR (400 MHz)

### Measurements & correlations
- **9 binary aqueous solutions** per system, gravimetrically prepared (KF-corrected for residual water: 0.03–0.05 wt%).
- **Liquid density (ρ)** at 288.15, 293.15, 298.15, 303.15, 308.15 K → **Connors–Wright model**.
- **Molar excess volume (Vₘᴱ)** → third-degree polynomials and **Redlich–Kister** expansions (degree chosen by F-test).
- **Dynamic viscosity (η)**: pure components → **VFTH (Vogel–Fulcher–Tammann–Hesse)**; binary solutions → **Arrhenius linearization** (288.15–308.15 K).
- **Ionicity / degree of dissociation (α)** → **Walden plot** at 298.15 K.
- For **[Ch][Ala]**, only refractive index was newly measured (other properties published in a prior group work).

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **Density (Connors–Wright fit)**: low standard deviations, 0.37 < σ_SD / kg·m⁻³ < 1.40.
- **Excess molar volume**: third-degree polynomials (0.0447 < σ_SD·10⁶ / m³·mol⁻¹ < 0.0729) and Redlich–Kister (0.040 < σ_SD·10⁶ < 0.082).
- **Viscosity (Arrhenius)**: 1.14 < σ_SD·10² < 2.32.
- **Ionicity (α)**: determined vs composition for each CAAIL from the Walden plot (good-ionic-liquid line reference) — quantifies the fraction of fully dissociated ions, relevant to performance in separations.
- Property trends are governed by the choice of amino-acid anion (alanine vs glutamate vs threonine) and by the dicationic stoichiometry of [Ch]₂[Glut].

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Purely physicochemical/thermodynamic characterization; **no biological or stabilization assay** (RNA/protein) performed here.
- Limited to three amino-acid anions and aqueous binary (IL + water) systems; ternary/biomolecule-containing systems not studied.
- Temperature window limited to 288.15–308.15 K (near-ambient).
- CAAIL known limitations (high viscosity, hygroscopicity, possible high-temperature instability) noted from literature but not deeply probed.

## 7. Glossary
- **CAAIL (Choline–Amino Acid Ionic Liquid)**: IL pairing the choline/cholinium cation with a deprotonated amino-acid anion; biobased, biodegradable, low-toxicity.
- **[Ch]₂[Glut]**: dicholinium glutamate; glutamate carries two carboxylate charges, so two cholinium cations per anion.
- **Ionicity / degree of dissociation (α)**: fraction of IL that dissociates into free ions in solution; lower α indicates ion pairing/aggregation.
- **Walden plot**: log(molar conductivity) vs log(fluidity); position relative to the "ideal KCl line" gauges ionicity.
- **Excess molar volume (Vₘᴱ)**: deviation of a solution's volume from ideal mixing; reflects molecular packing/interactions.
- **Connors–Wright model**: empirical correlation (originally for surface tension) here applied to density vs composition.
- **Redlich–Kister expansion**: polynomial series for fitting excess thermodynamic properties vs composition.
- **VFTH (Vogel–Fulcher–Tammann–Hesse)**: model for viscosity–temperature dependence of glass-forming/ionic liquids.
