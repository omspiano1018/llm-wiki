## One-line Summary
Critical perspective on ionic liquids (ILs) and deep eutectic solvents (DESs) as multifunctional platforms for stabilizing and delivering nucleic acid biopharmaceuticals (siRNA, mRNA, ASOs), including a SWOT analysis and future outlook.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First critical and integrative review focusing on physicochemical mechanisms by which ILs/DESs stabilize nucleic acids, rather than purely descriptive overview
- Establishes structure-property relationships linking IL/DES composition to nucleic acid stabilization efficacy
- Reviews cholinium- and ammonium-based ILs and cholinium-derived DESs for siRNA, ASO, and mRNA formulations
- Introduces concept of "IL-robed RNA" and surface-active ionic liquids (SAILs) that combine molecular protection with delivery capacity
- Includes SWOT analysis and forward-looking perspective on translational challenges
- Market context: nucleic acid therapeutics market ~USD 6.94 billion (2025), projected USD 12.24 billion by 2029 (CAGR 15.2%)

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Critical perspective/review combining mechanistic analysis, comparative evaluation, and SWOT framework
- Covers three nucleic acid classes: mRNA vaccines, siRNA therapies, antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs)
- Mechanistic focus: how IL/DES tunable ionic environments, hydrogen-bond networks, and physicochemical versatility preserve DNA/RNA structures
- Key protection mechanisms reviewed: mitigation of hydrolysis, oxidation, and nuclease activity
- Comparison with conventional excipients and established delivery systems (LNPs)
- Foundational work cited: Zakrewsky et al. (2014) on cholinium IL for siRNA transdermal delivery; Tateishi-Karimata & Sugimoto on IL stabilization of DNA/RNA structures

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Hydrated, biocompatible ILs and cholinium-based DESs stabilize duplex DNA, triplexes, and G-quadruplex structures with greater long-term stability than conventional aqueous buffers
- Cholinium-based ILs enhance transdermal transport and intracellular uptake while maintaining acceptable biocompatibility
- DESs form dense hydrogen-bonding networks that support preservation of nucleic acid structure and modulation of biomolecular interactions
- IL/DES systems can serve as API-ILs (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient Ionic Liquids) — multifunctional components integrating stabilization and delivery
- SAILs integrate protective and delivery roles in a single molecular entity

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Cytotoxicity remains a concern for many IL systems; biocompatible cholinium-based variants show promise but require further clinical validation
- Viscosity of ILs/DESs can hinder formulation scalability and injection
- No clear regulatory frameworks (FDA, EMA) exist for pharmaceutical use of IL/DES excipients
- Scalability of IL/DES synthesis and cost compared to established LNP components unclear
- More in vivo data needed for mRNA/DES formulations
- Greener synthesis routes (neoteric solvents, biobased components) needed for sustainable manufacturing

## 7. Glossary
- **Ionic liquid (IL)**: Salt with melting point below 100°C; composed of organic cation and organic/inorganic anion; tunable physicochemical properties
- **Deep eutectic solvent (DES)**: Mixture of hydrogen bond donor and acceptor forming a liquid at lower temperature than either component alone
- **Cholinium-based IL**: ILs with choline cation; generally biocompatible and biodegradable
- **SAIL (Surface-Active Ionic Liquid)**: IL with amphiphilic character enabling self-assembly and membrane interaction
- **API-IL (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient IL)**: IL designed so that one or both ions are pharmacologically active
- **siRNA**: Small interfering RNA; ~21-nt double-stranded RNA that silences target mRNA via RISC
- **ASO (Antisense Oligonucleotide)**: Short single-stranded nucleic acid complementary to target RNA
- **SWOT**: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats — strategic analysis framework
