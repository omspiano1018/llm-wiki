## One-line Summary
Experimental design of a biocompatible propionyl-choline-chloride ionic liquid ([Ch] IL) that binds and stabilizes DNA, protects it from nucleases, enables long-term storage, and simultaneously boosts aqueous solubility and cellular uptake of doxorubicin — with citric acid added to keep the IL–DNA complex positively charged (stable) at physiological pH.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First **multi-functional choline-based IL** reported to combine (a) nucleic-acid (DNA) binding/stabilization and (b) improved cellular uptake of a drug in one system
- Identifies and **solves the physiological-pH instability problem**: [Ch] IL has positive zeta potential and complexes DNA, but at physiological pH the complex's zeta potential turns negative and dissociates → adding **citric acid (CA)** raises zeta potential and restores strong, stable complexation
- DNA can be **retrieved from complexes with preserved purity and integrity** (reversible binding → usable for storage)
- Demonstrates dual utility: long-term nucleic-acid storage **and** in vivo drug-permeation enhancement

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Synthesis**: [Ch] IL = propionyl choline chloride, from choline chloride + propionyl chloride (1:1.5), 70 °C reflux 24 h in DCM, precipitated with cold diethyl ether. Amino-acid-modified variants (proline, glycine, alanine) made by salt metathesis (Ag₂O → remove AgCl → add amino acid → lyophilize).
- **Characterization**: FT-IR (bonds), Malvern zeta sizer (size + zeta potential).
- **DNA complexation assays**: agarose electrophoresis, ethidium-bromide dye-displacement, UV absorbance; effect of pH on complex stability; DNase protection assay.
- **Drug/cell assays**: doxorubicin solubilization; cellular uptake; biocompatibility via MTT cell proliferation and hemolysis; tested on MCF-7 breast cancer cells. Plasmid DNA prepared by CsCl method.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- **[Ch] IL** forms effective IL–DNA complexes (positive zeta potential), but complex **destabilizes at physiological pH** (zeta → negative)
- **[CA-Ch] IL** (citric acid added) → increased zeta potential → **stable IL–DNA complexes** at physiological pH
- **Nuclease protection**: IL–DNA complex protects DNA even in a DNase-rich environment; provides long-term stability
- **Reversibility**: DNA retrievable from complexes without loss of purity/integrity
- **Drug delivery**: [Ch] IL improves doxorubicin aqueous solubility and **enhances its cellular uptake in a concentration-dependent manner**
- **Biocompatibility**: both [Ch] IL and [CA-Ch] IL pass cell-proliferation and hemolysis tests

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Demonstrated on **DNA (plasmid)**, not directly on RNA — RNA translation/stability not tested (relevant caveat for an RNA-delivery program)
- Drug-delivery proof uses doxorubicin (small molecule), not a nucleic-acid cargo delivered into cells; transfection/knockdown not the endpoint
- In vivo permeation claimed as application but main data are in vitro
- Optimal ion/CA ratios and scale-up not deeply explored

## 7. Glossary
- **[Ch] IL**: propionyl choline chloride ionic liquid (this paper's lead compound)
- **[CA-Ch] IL**: the [Ch] IL with citric acid added to stabilize the IL–DNA complex at physiological pH
- **Zeta potential**: surface charge of a particle/complex in suspension; positive values here drive DNA binding and cell-surface association
- **Dye-displacement assay**: ethidium-bromide displacement used to confirm IL binding to DNA
- **Salt metathesis**: anion-exchange reaction (here via Ag₂O) used to make amino-acid-modified ILs
- **DNase protection assay**: tests whether complexation shields DNA from nuclease degradation
