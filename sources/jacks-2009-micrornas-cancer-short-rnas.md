## One-line Summary
An essay reviewing the evidence linking microRNAs to cancer biology, cataloging oncogenic and tumor suppressor miRNAs, describing the genetic and epigenetic alterations affecting miRNA genes in tumors, and discussing the mechanistic basis of miRNA-driven tumorigenesis.

## 1. Document Information
- **Title**: MicroRNAs and Cancer: Short RNAs Go a Long Way
- **Authors**: Andrea Ventura (Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center) and Tyler Jacks (Koch Institute, MIT; HHMI)
- **Year**: 2009
- **Journal**: Cell, Vol. 136, pp. 586–591
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.cell.2009.02.005
- **Type**: Essay (Cell RNA special issue)

## 2. Key Contributions
- Systematically catalogs genetic and epigenetic mechanisms by which miRNAs contribute to tumorigenesis (deletion, amplification, point mutations, methylation silencing, 3'UTR mutations)
- Reviews the founding discovery of miRNA-cancer connections: miR-15a/16-1 deletion in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (Calin/Croce, 2002)
- Documents oncogenic miRNAs: miR-155 (lymphomagenesis, cooperates with Myc; regulates AID in B cells) and miR-17~92 cluster (B cell lymphoma, cooperates with Myc)
- Documents tumor suppressor miRNAs: let-7 (targets RAS; deletion promotes lung tumorigenesis), miR-34 family (p53 target; pro-apoptotic)
- Discusses germline versus somatic miRNA mutations and their functional consequences
- Establishes framework for miRNA "oncogene" vs. "tumor suppressor" classification

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Essay synthesizing human cancer genomics data, cell line studies, and genetically engineered mouse models (GEMMs)
- miRNA mechanism: seed sequence (nt 2–8) base pairs with seed-match in target 3'UTR; inhibits translation and reduces mRNA stability; decreased expression of target protein
- Tumor-promoting mechanisms: overexpression/amplification of miRNA targeting tumor suppressors; deletion/methylation of miRNA normally repressing oncogenes; 3'UTR mutations in oncogenes eliminating miRNA-mediated repression (HMGA-2 example)
- Key experimental systems: retroviral transduction of miRNAs into mouse bone marrow (miR-155 leukemia), transgenic mice overexpressing miR-17~92 in lymphocytes, conditional knockout mice for miR-155 and miR-17~92

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- miR-15a/16-1: frequently deleted in CLL; first miRNA-cancer connection established by Calin/Croce (2002)
- miR-155: upregulated in hematopoietic malignancies, breast, lung, pancreas; maps to BIC oncogene locus; ectopic expression induces pre-B cell leukemia or myeloproliferation depending on expression system; knockout mice show defective B and T cell function, reduced germinal center reaction; targets AID (activation-induced cytidine deaminase)
- miR-17~92 cluster: 6 miRNAs from single transcript; amplified in B cell lymphomas; cooperates with c-Myc to accelerate lymphomagenesis; transgenic overexpression causes lymphoproliferative disorder; homozygous knockout causes premature B cell death at pro-B/pre-B stage; candidate target: Bim (pro-apoptotic)
- let-7 family: targets RAS oncogene family; downregulated in lung cancer; let-7 expression inversely correlates with RAS levels in lung tumors
- miR-34 family: direct transcriptional targets of p53; promote apoptosis; frequently epigenetically silenced in cancer
- p53-miR-34 circuit: miR-34a/b/c transcription activated by p53; miR-34 targets include CDK4, CDK6, E2F3 (cell cycle); links miRNA pathway to major tumor suppressor network

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- The full spectrum of physiologically relevant miRNA targets for most cancer-associated miRNAs remains unknown
- Most documented mechanisms are from cell lines and mouse models; translating to human cancer causal mechanisms is ongoing
- Many potential mechanisms (e.g., point mutations reducing miRNA processing efficiency, gain of miRNA binding in tumor suppressors) have no known examples yet
- Single miRNA knockouts may show incomplete phenotypic overlap with the full cancer phenotype because miRNAs regulate multiple targets
- Therapeutic exploitation of miRNA biology in cancer requires delivery and specificity challenges to be resolved

## 7. Glossary
- **miRNA**: MicroRNA; ~22 nt RNA that represses gene expression by base-pairing with 3'UTR of target mRNAs
- **Seed sequence**: Nucleotides 2–8 of the miRNA; critical for target recognition
- **Oncomiр**: Oncogenic miRNA; promotes tumorigenesis by repressing tumor suppressor genes
- **miR-155**: Oncogenic miRNA; upregulated in many hematopoietic malignancies; maps to BIC locus; regulates AID in B cells
- **miR-17~92**: Oncogenic miRNA cluster of 6 miRNAs; amplified in B cell lymphomas; cooperates with Myc
- **let-7**: Tumor suppressor miRNA family; targets RAS; downregulated in lung cancer
- **miR-34**: Tumor suppressor miRNA family; transcriptional targets of p53; pro-apoptotic; frequently silenced by methylation
- **AID**: Activation-induced cytidine deaminase; target of miR-155; mediates somatic hypermutation and class-switch recombination in B cells
- **CLL**: Chronic lymphocytic leukemia; tumor type in which miR-15a/16-1 deletion was first linked to cancer
- **miR-15a/16-1**: miRNA cluster at 13q14; frequently deleted in CLL; targets BCL2 anti-apoptotic gene
- **BIC**: B-cell integration cluster; original name for the locus encoding miR-155 primary transcript; common proviral insertion site in avian lymphomas
- **HMGA-2**: High-mobility group A2; oncogene whose 3'UTR contains let-7 binding sites; translocations removing these sites lead to oncogenic HMGA-2 overexpression
- **GEMMs**: Genetically engineered mouse models; important tool for validating miRNA function in cancer
