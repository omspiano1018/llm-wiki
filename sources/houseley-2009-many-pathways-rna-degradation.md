## One-line Summary
Comprehensive review of the conserved and divergent mechanisms of RNA degradation across bacteria, archaea, and eukaryotes, covering the major enzymatic machinery, cofactors, surveillance pathways, and small RNA-mediated degradation.

## 1. Document Information

## 2. Key Contributions
- Establishes that RNA degradation is universal and that despite enormous pathway complexity, basic mechanisms (endonucleases, 5' and 3' exonucleases, cofactor-assisted degradation) are conserved across all life.
- Introduces the concept that many RNases are multifunctional—participating in both RNA processing (generating precise mature ends) and RNA degradation (complete destruction).
- Details the TRAMP complex (poly(A) polymerase + zinc-knuckle + helicase) as the major nuclear RNA surveillance cofactor in yeast, analogous to polyubiquitylation targeting proteins to the proteasome.
- Describes how the eukaryotic exosome complex and the bacterial degradosome represent structurally and functionally analogous multi-enzyme degradation machines.
- Reviews roles of small RNAs (miRNAs, siRNAs, piRNAs) in directing sequence-specific mRNA degradation, connecting RNA silencing to the broader degradation machinery.
- Provides a comprehensive Table 1 mapping yeast RNA degradation factors to their human homologs.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Major RNase classes**: (1) Endonucleases (internal cleavage); (2) 5' exonucleases (Rat1/XRN2 in nucleus; Xrn1 in cytoplasm); (3) 3' exonucleases (exosome complex).
- **Exosome**: Ring-shaped core of 9–10 subunits (Rrp41, Rrp42, etc.) with a single catalytic subunit (Rrp44/Dis3) providing 3' hydrolytic exonuclease and endonuclease; nuclear exosome has additional Rrp6.
- **TRAMP complex**: Nuclear RNA surveillance/degradation complex in yeast; contains Trf4 or Trf5 (poly(A) polymerase), Air1 or Air2 (zinc-knuckle RNA binder), and Mtr4 (helicase); tags defective RNAs with short poly(A) tails for exosome degradation.
- **Bacterial degradosome**: PNPase (3' exonuclease/polymerase) + RNase E (endonuclease) + RhlB (helicase) + enolase; degrades mRNA in E. coli.
- **Cofactors**:
  - Helicases: unwind RNA secondary structures or displace proteins; Mtr4 and Ski2 with exosome; RhlB with degradosome.
  - Polymerases (polyadenylation): TRAMP adds short poly(A) tails as "landing pads" for 3' exonucleases; E. coli PAP similarly stimulates degradation.
  - Poly(U) polymerases: in humans, poly(U) tails trigger histone mRNA degradation via Lsm1-7 complex.
  - Lsm complexes: Lsm1-7 (cytoplasmic, mRNA decay) and Lsm2-8 (nuclear, snRNA processing); Hfq (bacterial homolog).
- **Decapping**: Dcp2 (catalytic) + Dcp1 removes the 5' cap, exposing mRNA to Xrn1; decapping is stimulated by deadenylation (Ccr4-NOT complex removes poly(A) tail).
- **mRNA decay pathways in eukaryotes**:
  - Deadenylation-dependent: poly(A) removal by Ccr4-NOT → decapping → Xrn1 (5'→3') or exosome (3'→5').
  - Deadenylation-independent: AU-rich element (ARE)-mediated rapid decay.
  - NMD: EJC-dependent pathway; PTC triggers Upf1/2/3-mediated decapping and degradation.
  - NSD: No-stop decay; ribosomes stalling at 3' end trigger Ski-exosome degradation.
  - NGD: No-go decay; stalled ribosomes trigger endonucleolytic cleavage.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- 5' exonucleases (Xrn1 family) were believed absent from bacteria until de la Sierra-Gallay et al. (2008) and Mathy et al. (2007) identified bacterial 5' exonucleases, establishing this activity in all domains.
- TRAMP-exosome combination degrades: aberrant ribosomal RNA processing intermediates, cryptic unstable transcripts (CUTs), hypomodified tRNAs, and defective pre-mRNAs.
- Human homologs of all TRAMP components exist (POLS/Trf4, PAPD5/Trf5, ZCCHC3/Air, SKIV2L2/Mtr4), though a functional human TRAMP complex was not yet demonstrated.
- Poly(U) tails stimulate degradation of histone mRNAs (Mullen and Marzluff, 2008) via Lsm1-7, representing a distinct pathway from polyadenylation-based decay.
- The evolutionary model: ancestral RNA degradation stimulated by heteropolymeric 3' tail addition (by PNPase/archaeal exosome) predates the emergence of dedicated poly(A) polymerases; nuclear polyadenylation-for-degradation likely represents the ancestral function.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Specificity determinants for RNA degradation—how the cell distinguishes aberrant from normal RNAs—remained the most pressing question.
- A functional human TRAMP complex had not been demonstrated biochemically.
- The full extent of regulated mRNA decay (as opposed to constitutive turnover) was not catalogued.
- Connections between RNA degradation pathways and RNA modifications (e.g., N6-methyladenosine) were unexplored at this time.

## 7. Glossary
- **Exosome**: Multi-subunit 3' exonuclease/endonuclease complex; present in archaea and eukaryotes; nuclear and cytoplasmic forms.
- **TRAMP complex**: Nuclear poly(A) polymerase + zinc-knuckle + helicase; tags defective RNAs for exosome degradation.
- **Xrn1**: Major cytoplasmic 5'→3' exonuclease in eukaryotes; degrades decapped mRNAs.
- **Rat1/XRN2**: Nuclear 5' exonuclease; involved in transcription termination and RNA processing.
- **Dcp1/Dcp2**: mRNA decapping complex; removes 5' cap to expose RNA to Xrn1.
- **Ccr4-NOT**: Major cytoplasmic deadenylase complex; initiates mRNA decay by removing poly(A) tail.
- **PNPase**: Polynucleotide phosphorylase; bacterial 3' exonuclease that can also polymerize RNA; homolog of eukaryotic exosome subunits.
- **Degradosome**: Bacterial multi-enzyme RNA degradation complex (PNPase + RNase E + RhlB + enolase).
- **Lsm1-7**: Cytoplasmic Lsm ring complex; promotes mRNA decapping and interacts with poly(U) tails.
- **Hfq**: Bacterial Sm-like protein; RNA chaperone essential for sRNA function; bacterial analog of Lsm proteins.
- **NMD**: Nonsense-mediated decay; surveillance pathway degrading mRNAs with premature stop codons.
- **CUTs**: Cryptic unstable transcripts; short, unstable pervasive transcripts in yeast degraded by TRAMP-exosome.
- **ARE**: AU-rich element; sequence in mRNA 3'UTR that promotes rapid mRNA deadenylation and decay.
