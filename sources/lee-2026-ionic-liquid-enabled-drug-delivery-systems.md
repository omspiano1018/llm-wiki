## One-line Summary
Framework-driven review positioning ionic liquids (ILs) as formulation-enabling materials (not standalone therapeutics) across nanocarrier, microtechnology, and biomacromolecule drug-delivery platforms, organized by platform rather than route, with a critical assessment of structure-dependent toxicity, evaluation/scalability gaps, and regulatory ambiguity.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Positions ILs as **formulation-enabling materials** rather than standalone therapeutics, with a selective, framework-driven (not exhaustive) organization
- Organizes IL-enabled DDS **by delivery platform** (nanocarriers, microtechnology, biomacromolecules) rather than by administration route, treating route as a contextual variable
- Synthesizes **structure–property–function** and **structure–activity–toxicity** relationships, framing IL–membrane interaction as the common driver of both delivery enhancement and antimicrobial/toxicity effects
- Provides a **three-generation conceptual classification** of ILs (solvent-oriented → task-specific → biocompatibility/application-oriented) as an analytical (not strictly chronological) framework
- Critically consolidates translational barriers: toxicity, lack of standardized evaluation criteria, scalability, and regulatory ambiguity
- Strictly defines ILs as room-temperature ionic salts and **excludes deep eutectic solvents (DESs)** as conceptually distinct (contrast with [[computational/verissimo-2026-ionic-liquids-deep-eutectic]] which covers both)
- Bibliometric/market context: ~66 publications up to 2013 vs. 456 during 2014–2021 (peak 83 in 2020); global ILs market ~USD 53.46M in 2023, projected CAGR 8.2% (2024–2030)

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
Narrative critical review. Three-part architecture:

**(A) IL generations (Section 2)** — analytical framework:
- **1st gen (solvent-oriented)**: imidazolium ([C4mim]+), pyridinium; anions [AlCl4]−, PF6−, BF4−. Origins: Walden 1914 (ethylammonium nitrate), dialkylimidazolium synthesis in 1980s. Limited by hydrolytic instability, poor biodegradability, aquatic toxicity, cost.
- **2nd gen (task-specific, TSILs)**: imidazolium, quaternary ammonium; PF6−, BF4−, TFSI−. Emphasis on moisture/air stability and functional control (biocatalysis, metal complexing). e.g. [C4C1Im][PF6] for pravadoline synthesis (90–94% yield).
- **3rd gen (biocompatibility/application-oriented)**: choline+, amino-acid-based cations; organic acid/amino-acid/API-derived anions (API-IL concept). Improved biocompatibility and formulation flexibility for DDS; limited long-term safety/regulatory data.

**(B) Platform-based DDS survey (Section 3)**:
- **Nanotechnology-enabled** — IL-integrated nanocarriers/nanoparticles, poly(ionic liquid) (PIL) nanoplatforms; ILs act as solubilizers, stabilizers, surface modifiers; EPR-based tumor targeting.
- **Microtechnology-assisted** — PIL microneedles (tunable mechanical strength + intrinsic antimicrobial), IL-based microemulsions (amphiphilic solvency, surfactant substitution).
- **Biomacromolecule-based** — proteins, nucleic acids, peptides, vaccines; ILs preserve native structure, resist enzymatic degradation, enhance intracellular delivery and storage stability.
- Route-specific subsections (transdermal, oral mucosal, GI, injectable) treated as contextual.

**(C) Antimicrobial properties (Section 4)** + **cross-cutting limitations (Section 5)**.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
**Nanocarrier examples (Table 2):**
- ImIL-PEG@MCM-41 (imidazolium IL + PEG, mesoporous silica) loaded with lapatinib: ~150 nm, EE ~91%, pH-responsive release (acidic tumor microenvironment)
- Rutin-loaded PLGA NPs with choline ILs ([Cho][Phe], [Cho][Glu]): 250–300 nm, ζ −40 mV, EE ≤76%, ~85% sustained release over 72 h, no HaCaT cytotoxicity
- PIL block copolymer NPs (P[VHim]NTf2) + doxorubicin: 40–80 nm, loading content up to ~70%, dual pH/UV-triggered release
- Imidazolium-IL/alginate/clay nanocomposite co-loaded with methotrexate + ciprofloxacin: ~70 nm, EE ~99%/98%, pH-responsive co-delivery

**Microtechnology examples (Table 3):**
- PIL microneedles loaded with salicylate via anion exchange: anti-inflammatory + antibacterial vs. *Cutibacterium acnes*
- NO-releasing imidazolium PIL microneedles: antifungal/antibiofilm, accelerated wound healing
- Imidazolium IL/o microemulsion for acyclovir: ~20 nm droplets, enhanced skin deposition; BMIMBr IL/o microemulsion for 5-fluorouracil: up to 4× dermal penetration

**Biomacromolecule examples (Table 5):**
- Choline-based ILs enhance **transdermal insulin** delivery while maintaining skin compatibility; IL-mediated nanovesicles for macromolecular transdermal transport
- Favipiravir-based API-IL: **78–125× aqueous solubility increase** and improved oral bioavailability vs. crystalline API
- [Ch][Tre]–[Ch][Ger] mixed IL for tretinoin: extreme apparent solubilization (~1.75 × 10^8-fold vs. water), 95–97% release at 5 min, increased oral Cmax/AUC
- Choline oleate ionic co-aggregates for injectable paclitaxel; protein stability maintained up to ~2 months at ambient / ≥4 months refrigerated

**Antimicrobial structure–activity (STARs):**
- Activity is membrane-active and structure-governed; increasing cation alkyl chain length beyond ~4 carbons lowers MIC (more hydrophobic, stronger membrane interaction)
- Imidazolium/pyridinium often more potent than quaternary ammonium; long-chain cholinium/imidazolium ILs especially active and antibiofilm, generally more so vs. Gram-positive (Gram-negative outer membrane confers tolerance)
- API-IL example: ampicillin-IL more active vs. resistant *E. coli* and MRSA than halide salt; long-chain imidazolium/pyrrolidinium ILs act as **colistin adjuvants** vs. Gram-negative bacteria
- Resistance considered slower to develop because membrane disruption lacks a single molecular target — but the same membrane activity drives off-target cytotoxicity (activity–toxicity trade-off)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- **Toxicity is structure-dependent** and correlates with the same alkyl-chain/lipophilicity trends that drive efficacy — a fundamental activity–toxicity trade-off; many ILs more toxic than baseline models predict; limited in vitro metabolism
- **Biodegradability/ecotoxicity** poorly characterized; low volatility ≠ low ecological risk; early-gen ILs persistent
- **No standardized evaluation criteria** for IL-DDS performance/safety
- **Scalability**: microneedle mechanical robustness and batch reproducibility; microemulsion sensitivity to component ratios; viscosity
- **Regulatory ambiguity**: ILs not yet classified within pharmaceutical frameworks; not established excipients — early regulatory alignment needed
- **Route-specific risks**: transdermal barrier disruption/irritation; GI tolerability; parenteral hemocompatibility/immunogenicity/impurity control
- Authors stress ILs are **not universally superior** — established polymeric/lipid carriers may remain preferable for regulatory maturity and manufacturing scalability
- Future: structure-guided design, standardized benchmarks, comprehensive safety profiling, early regulatory engagement

## 7. Glossary
- **Ionic liquid (IL)**: salt composed entirely of ions, liquid at/near room temperature (mp often <100 °C); bulky asymmetric organic cation + organic/inorganic anion
- **Poly(ionic liquid) (PIL)**: polymer with IL repeat units; provides tunable mechanical strength and intrinsic antimicrobial behavior (used in microneedles)
- **API-IL**: ionic liquid in which one/both ions are an active pharmaceutical ingredient
- **TSIL (task-specific IL)**: 2nd-gen IL engineered for a specific function via rational ion selection
- **DDS**: drug delivery system
- **STARs**: Structure–Activity–Toxicity Relationships — design framework balancing antimicrobial efficacy with biocompatibility/environmental safety
- **EPR**: enhanced permeability and retention — passive tumor accumulation of nanocarriers
- **SC**: stratum corneum — outermost skin barrier layer
- **CPE**: chemical penetration enhancer
- **DES (excluded here)**: deep eutectic solvent — hydrogen-bond donor/acceptor eutectic mixture; treated as conceptually distinct from ILs
