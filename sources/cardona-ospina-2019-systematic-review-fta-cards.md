## One-line Summary
PRISMA systematic review of 47 studies evaluating FTA cards for viral RNA preservation and fieldwork transport, concluding they are effective for RNA virus storage and detection but may not consistently inactivate infectious virus.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First PRISMA-compliant systematic review specifically evaluating FTA cards for viral RNA preservation
- Covers 47 studies from MEDLINE (PubMed), Scopus, and Web of Science (search: "FTA cards" AND "RNA")
- Summarizes three distinct RNA extraction methods from FTA cards used across studies
- Identifies key variable factors: storage temperature, duration, amplicon size, extraction method
- Critical finding: biosafety concern — only 14 of 47 studies tested virus inactivation; in one case virus remained infective on FTA card
- Animal samples accounted for 38.3% of publications (poultry, wild birds, suids, bovids); remaining human/plant

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- PRISMA systematic review (Moher et al. 2009)
- Search date: June 2018; databases: MEDLINE (PubMed), Scopus, Web of Science
- Search strategy: "FTA cards" AND "RNA"; no year limit; English and Spanish only
- Screening: title/abstract by 2 independent reviewers; full-text by 2 reviewers; disagreements resolved by third party
- Inclusion criteria: studies using FTA cards for storage and later isolation of RNA of viral origin with subsequent amplification
- Exclusion: opinion articles, letters without original data, reviews, inaccessible full texts
- Quality assessment: custom 10-item checklist scoring completeness of methods (0-2 scale per item)
- Items scored: sample source, time of storage, temperature, amplicon size, extraction method, purification method, cDNA synthesis conditions, PCR conditions, RNA quantification, sequencing confirmation
- Success definition: targeted RNA fragment amplified from cDNA (RT-PCR) or RNA isolation enabling NGS

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- 175 records retrieved; 11 additional from reference checking; 47 articles included after screening
- All 47 included articles achieved viral RNA amplification from FTA cards
- 95.7% of studies extracted total RNA before targeted amplification; 4.3% isolated viral RNA directly
- Three extraction methods documented (specific protocols not named in extracted text; details in full paper)
- Variables across studies: storage temperatures (RT to refrigerator); storage durations (days to years); amplicon sizes (variable)
- Inactivation testing: only 14/47 studies assessed virus infectivity; in 1 case, virus remained infective — biosafety concern for fieldwork handling
- Animal virus focus: avian influenza, swine viruses, bovine viruses (RNA viruses in agricultural/wildlife settings)
- FTA card advantages: room-temperature storage, no cold chain, easy field collection, lyse and inactivate most organisms

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Custom quality assessment tool (not previously validated); scoring somewhat subjective
- Extensive variability in protocols across 47 studies makes direct comparison difficult
- Most studies not powered to detect rare failures in RNA preservation or inactivation
- Optimal storage temperature and maximum storage duration not definitively established
- Biosafety: dried blood spots should be considered potentially infectious unless specifically treated for viral inactivation
- Future: standardized protocols for FTA card RNA extraction; rigorous inactivation validation for each virus type; direct quantitative comparison with alternative RT methods

## 6. Related Work
- Lou et al. (2014): room temperature storage review comparing FTA cards with other modalities (chemical matrices, lyophilization)
- Fabre et al. (2014): alternative anhydrous capsule approach for room-temperature RNA storage
- GE Healthcare/Whatman: FTA card technology documentation
- Hsiao et al. (1999); Smith and Burgoyne (2004): foundational FTA card DNA preservation studies
- Avian influenza biosafety literature (Kraus et al. 2011): FTA cards proposed for improved biosafety in avian influenza transport

## 7. Glossary
- **FTA card (Flinders Technology Associates)**: Filter paper impregnated with chemical mixture (denaturants, free radical scavenger) that lyses cells, inactivates bacteria/proteins, and preserves nucleic acids at room temperature
- **PRISMA**: Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses; standardized reporting framework
- **RT-PCR**: Reverse Transcription PCR; first converts RNA to cDNA then amplifies; gold standard for viral RNA detection
- **RT-LAMP**: Reverse Transcription Loop-Mediated Isothermal Amplification; rapid point-of-care alternative to RT-PCR
- **Dried blood spot (DBS)**: Sample collected on FTA card; should be treated as potentially infectious
- **Amplicon**: PCR product; amplicon size is a quality indicator (larger amplicons require better preserved RNA)
- **cDNA**: Complementary DNA synthesized from RNA by reverse transcriptase; intermediate for PCR from RNA templates
- **NGS**: Next-generation sequencing; high-throughput sequencing platform enabling whole-genome characterization
