## One-line Summary
Review of how mutations in RNA sequences, RNA-binding proteins, and the splicing machinery cause human diseases, with particular focus on alternative splicing defects, and an overview of RNA-based therapeutic strategies.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Provides a comprehensive catalogue (Table 1) of human diseases caused by mutations affecting RNA-dependent functions across diverse RNA classes: snRNAs, snoRNAs, rRNAs, tRNAs, miRNAs, and mRNA splicing factors.
- Explains that alternative splicing of most human genes (~90%+ of multi-exon genes) creates enormous opportunities for disease-causing mis-splicing.
- Distinguishes cis-acting mutations (within the affected gene's pre-mRNA) from trans-acting mutations (in splicing machinery/regulatory proteins affecting many transcripts).
- Discusses RNA gain-of-function diseases (myotonic dystrophy types 1 and 2) where expanded repeat RNA sequesters essential splicing regulators.
- Reviews emerging RNA therapeutics: antisense oligonucleotides, modified U1 snRNAs, trans-splicing, and RNAi for disease treatment.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- **Splicing mechanism**: Spliceosome assembled from five snRNPs (U1, U2, U4/U6, U5) and >100 protein factors; minor spliceosome uses U11, U12, U4atac/U6atac. Sm core of seven proteins shared by snRNPs (except U6).
- **Splicing code**: Loosely defined consensus splice-site sequences plus exonic and intronic splicing enhancers (ESEs) and silencers (ESSs) recognized by SR proteins (positive) and hnRNP proteins (negative).
- **SMN complex**: ATP-dependent assemblyosome (SMN + Gemins 2–8 + Unrip) that methylates Sm proteins (via methylosome/PRMT5) and assembles snRNP Sm cores.
- **cis-acting mutations**: Point mutations in splice sites or regulatory elements; examples include BRCA1, CFTR, TTR (transthyretin), MAPT (tau).
- **trans-acting mutations**: Mutations in splicing factors; retinitis pigmentosa (PRPF31, PRPF8, HPRP3, PAP1), ALS (TARDBP/TDP-43).
- **RNA gain-of-function (DM1/DM2)**: Expanded CUG repeats (DMPK) or CCUG repeats (ZNF9) form RNA foci that sequester MBNL splicing regulators, causing widespread mis-splicing.
- **RNA therapeutics**: Antisense oligonucleotides to block cryptic splice sites, redirect splicing, or correct frameshifts; modified snRNAs to compensate for splice-site mutations.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Deep sequencing data suggest >90% of human multi-exon genes are alternatively spliced, with isoform diversity far exceeding genome size.
- Titin (316 exons), ryanodine receptor 1 (106 exons), and dystrophin (79 exons) are extreme examples of genes subject to complex splicing with many disease-associated mutations.
- SMN2 missense mutation (C→T in exon 7) creates an hnRNP A1 binding site and destroys an ESE, causing exon 7 skipping; insufficient full-length SMN protein causes spinal muscular atrophy (SMA).
- DM1 CUG-expanded DMPK transcripts form nuclear foci sequestering MBNL proteins, causing fetal splicing patterns to persist in adult tissues (e.g., cardiac troponin T, insulin receptor).
- Antisense oligonucleotides directed at aberrant splice sites in IKBKAP (familial dysautonomia) or SMN2 (SMA) partially restore correct splicing in cell culture and animal models.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- The "splicing code" for predicting which isoform is produced in a given cellular context from sequence alone was not solved.
- RNAi-based therapies faced challenges of delivery and off-target effects.
- Mechanisms by which TDP-43 and FUS (ALS-associated RBPs) cause disease were incompletely understood.
- Connections between alternative splicing regulation and cellular signaling pathways needed deeper characterization.

## 7. Glossary
- **RNP**: Ribonucleoprotein complex; the functional form of most RNA molecules in cells.
- **snRNP**: Small nuclear RNP; U1, U2, U4/U6, U5 snRNPs are core spliceosome components.
- **Spliceosome**: Large (~5 MDa) RNP machine that carries out pre-mRNA splicing.
- **SR proteins**: Serine-arginine-rich splicing regulators; generally promote exon definition and splicing.
- **hnRNP**: Heterogeneous nuclear RNP proteins; often act as splicing silencers.
- **ESE/ESS**: Exonic splicing enhancer/silencer; cis-acting sequence elements that regulate alternative splicing.
- **SMN**: Survival of motor neurons protein; catalytic core of the snRNP assembly complex; deficient in SMA.
- **SMA**: Spinal muscular atrophy; caused by insufficient SMN protein due to homozygous SMN1 deletion and inadequate SMN2 exon 7 inclusion.
- **DM1/DM2**: Myotonic dystrophy types 1 and 2; RNA gain-of-function diseases caused by expanded CUG (DMPK) or CCUG (ZNF9) repeats that sequester MBNL splicing factors.
- **MBNL**: Muscleblind-like protein; splicing regulator sequestered by expanded repeat RNAs in myotonic dystrophy.
- **TDP-43**: TAR DNA-binding protein 43; RBP mutated in ALS; involved in splicing and mRNA stability.
- **ASO**: Antisense oligonucleotide; short modified DNA/RNA used to modulate splicing or gene expression therapeutically.
