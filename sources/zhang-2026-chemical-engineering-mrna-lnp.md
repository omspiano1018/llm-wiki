## One-line Summary
Systematic review of chemical engineering strategies to improve mRNA stability and mRNA-LNP formulation, covering nucleotide modifications, terminal and backbone engineering, codon optimization, circular RNA, self-amplifying RNA, LNP design, and storage/transport solutions.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Comprehensive framework linking intrinsic mRNA sequence features and chemical modifications to stability outcomes
- Reviews 5' cap modification strategies (cap analogs), poly(A) tail engineering, UTR optimization, codon refinement
- Covers reversible 2'-OH acylation as a novel approach to protect the primary degradation site
- Reviews circular RNA constructs as inherently exonuclease-resistant alternatives
- Reviews self-amplifying RNA (saRNA) systems enabling lower mRNA doses through self-replication
- Discusses LNP formulation advances: low-immunogenicity LNPs, thermostable LNPs
- Covers storage and transport solutions: lyophilization, spray drying, and their role in extending shelf life

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Narrative review organized around: (1) mRNA stability determinants, (2) nucleotide/sequence engineering strategies, (3) LNP formulation advances, (4) storage/transport innovations, (5) clinical translation challenges
- mRNA stability determinants: intrinsic sequence features (GC content, codon usage, stem-loop structures), RNase activity (exoribonucleases, endoribonucleases), physicochemical factors (ionic fluctuations, oxidative conditions, pH variation)
- Exoribonucleases: 5'→3' (XRN1 after decapping) or 3'→5' (exosome complex after deadenylation); deadenylation-dependent and independent pathways (NMD, NSD)
- Endoribonucleases: Argonaute (AGO), SMG6; guided by miRNAs/siRNAs for sequence-specific cleavage
- PABPC1 protects poly(A) tails; RNA helicases can displace it, accelerating decay
- GC pairs: stronger triple hydrogen bonds than AU pairs; excessive GC can impede translation initiation
- Reversible 2'-OH acylation: chemical protection of primary hydrolysis site; requires downstream deprotection step

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Codon optimization: codons aligned with abundant tRNA pools enhance translation elongation, reducing ribosome stalling and nuclease exposure
- Stem-loop and hairpin structures: dual role (protective mask vs. degradation recognition motif) depending on sequence context and position
- 5' cap modification: standard m7G cap; anti-reverse cap analogs (ARCA) improve translational efficiency and stability
- Poly(A) tail: minimum ~120 nt optimal for stability and translation; extensions beyond 250+ nt may reduce efficiency
- N1-methylpseudouridine (m1Ψ): reduces innate immune sensing, improves stability; central to COVID-19 vaccine success
- Circular RNA: closed loop structure eliminates free 5'/3' termini that are attack points for exonucleases; growing interest for sustained expression
- saRNA: encodes RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) for self-amplification; allows 10-100x dose reduction
- Thermostable LNPs: achieved through lipid engineering (higher Tm lipids, novel ionizable lipids); extended refrigerator stability
- Lyophilization and spray drying extended shelf life by eliminating liquid water phase

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Reversible 2'-OH acylation requires a deprotection step, limiting practical in vivo application
- circRNA production at scale remains technically challenging
- saRNA amplification may trigger innate immune responses; careful RdRp selection and immunosuppression strategies needed
- LNP immunogenicity from PEG-lipid and ionizable lipid components; low-immunogenicity LNPs still being optimized
- Clinical translation challenges: regulatory pathways for novel nucleotide modifications and novel LNP compositions
- Combination strategies (chemical modification + sequence optimization + novel LNP + drying) likely needed for optimal outcomes

## 7. Glossary
- **NMD (nonsense-mediated decay)**: Quality control pathway degrading mRNAs with premature stop codons; 5'→3' degradation
- **NSD (nonstop decay)**: Pathway degrading mRNAs lacking stop codons via 3'→5' exosomal degradation
- **ARCA (anti-reverse cap analog)**: Modified 5' cap that ensures correct orientation during IVT and improves translation
- **Reversible 2'-OH acylation**: Chemical protection of 2'-OH groups with acyl groups; reduces hydrolysis during storage; removed in cells
- **circRNA**: Covalently closed circular RNA; no free 5'/3' termini; resistant to exonuclease degradation
- **saRNA (self-amplifying RNA)**: mRNA encoding viral RdRp that amplifies the RNA replicon in cells; enables dose reduction
- **RdRp (RNA-dependent RNA polymerase)**: Viral enzyme that replicates RNA from RNA template; used in saRNA systems
- **XRN1**: Major cytoplasmic 5'→3' exoribonuclease in eukaryotes; primary mRNA decay enzyme after decapping
- **Exosome complex**: Multi-subunit 3'→5' exoribonuclease complex; degrades deadenylated mRNAs
