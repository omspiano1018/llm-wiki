## One-line Summary
First report of CAGE clinical translation: a kilogram-scale CAGE1:2 gel (CGB400) passed seven translational steps — including GLP toxicology and a Phase 1b human study — showing marked reduction of inflammatory rosacea lesions in 26 patients.

## 1. Document Information
- **Journal**: Bioengineering & Translational Medicine, 6: e10191
- **Published**: 2021 (accepted October 2020)
- **Type**: Research report

## 2. Key Contributions
- First end-to-end clinical translation report for an ionic liquid/DES in dermatology
- Describes "seven steps of translation": scale-up, characterisation, stability, mechanism of action, dose determination, GLP toxicity, human clinical study
- Identifies unique challenges arising from the DES nature of CAGE (no covalent bond; characterisation methods in infancy)
- Reports Phase 1b cosmetic study: 12-week open-label, 26 patients, mild-moderate facial rosacea

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- CGB400 = 40% w/w CAGE1:2 gel in propylene glycol, d-limonene, hydroxypropyl cellulose
- Scale-up: one-step salt metathesis at kilogram scale in commercial reactor; quality defined by water content, pH (~8.5), conductivity (~1.3 mS/cm), appearance
- Characterisation: 1H NMR, HPLC, MDSC (Tg ≈ −68°C), TGA, FTIR
- Mechanism: CAGE extracts stratum corneum lipids, penetrates skin, neutralises pathogens (including S. epidermidis), modulates TLR2 signalling, inhibits KLK5
- GLP toxicology performed in minipigs before human study

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Scale-up: no critical issues; batch sizes >3 kg achieved
- Stability: CAGE1:2 stable under standard pharmaceutical storage conditions
- Phase 1b (n=26, 12 weeks): marked reduction in number of inflammatory lesions
- No preservatives needed — CAGE itself provides antimicrobial protection (paraben-free)
- First ionic liquid-based approach to complete clinical translation for a dermatological condition

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Open-label design (no placebo control) in Phase 1b; Phase 2 RCT needed
- Characterisation methods for DES drug products remain immature
- Mechanism (TLR2, KLK5) proposed but not fully elucidated in this paper

## 6. Related Work
- Mitragotri 2024 — broader review of all CAGE applications including Phase 2 Atopic Dermatitis
- Greene 2019 — antibiofilm scope and mechanism of CAGE

## 7. Glossary
- **CGB400**: Clinical formulation of CAGE1:2 at 40% w/w in gel base
- **DES**: Deep eutectic solvent
- **GLP**: Good Laboratory Practice (regulatory-grade toxicology studies)
- **TLR2**: Toll-like receptor 2 — innate immune signalling involved in rosacea pathophysiology
- **KLK5**: Kallikrein 5 — serine protease generating pro-inflammatory peptides in skin
- **MDSC**: Modulated differential scanning calorimetry
