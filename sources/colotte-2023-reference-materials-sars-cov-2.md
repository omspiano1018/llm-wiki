## One-line Summary
Validation study demonstrating that Imagene RNAshells encapsulated synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA controls remain functional for RT-PCR and RT-LAMP COVID-19 detection after room temperature storage equivalent to 10 years, distributed across five independent laboratories.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Validates multi-laboratory fitness-for-purpose of RNAshells-encapsulated SARS-CoV-2 synthetic RNA controls
- Demonstrates RNA stability equivalent to 10-year room temperature storage using accelerated aging (90°C, 16 hours; Arrhenius equation from Fabre et al. 2014)
- Tests detection across 3 SARS-CoV-2 RNA variants, 5 gene targets (E, N, RdRp, Sarbeco-E, Orf1a/b), 4 PCR thermocyclers, 6 PCR kits, and 1 RT-LAMP procedure
- Demonstrates eco-friendly room temperature shipping vs. costly dry ice transport
- Confirms that Imagene's anhydrous argon/helium encapsulation technology (vacuum-dried RNA in metallic capsules) protects RNA against humidity, oxidation, and temperature fluctuation

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Workflow: Twist Bioscience synthetic SARS-CoV-2 RNA controls → Imagene RNAshells encapsulation → accelerated aging subset → distribution to 5 laboratories → RT-qPCR and RT-LAMP analysis
- RNA controls: 3 variants (Australia/VIC01/2020 [RNA 1], Wuhan-Hu-1 [RNA 2], France/HF2393/2020 [RNA 7]); each consists of 6 non-overlapping 5 kb fragments covering >99% viral genome; ssRNA in vitro transcribed from synthetic DNA
- Encapsulation: 10 µL aliquots at ~10^4 or 10^5 copies/capsule; vacuum-dried with Imagene proprietary stabilization solution; sealed under anhydrous argon/helium; stored as RNAshells
- Accelerated aging: 90°C for 16 hours = 10-year equivalent at 25°C (Arrhenius model, Ea = 28.5 kcal/mol; from Fabre et al. 2014)
- Opening: ShellOpener tool; RNA rehydrated in RNAse-free water for 10-20 minutes
- Detection: RT-qPCR (4 thermocyclers, 6 kits) and RT-LAMP; targets: E gene, N gene, RdRp gene, Sarbeco-E, Orf1a/b

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- RNA retrieval was satisfactory across all laboratories and methods
- Detection was reproducible across 4 thermocyclers and 6 PCR kits
- Results from 10-year accelerated aged samples (90°C/16h) were not significantly different from unaged samples
- Room temperature stability demonstrated for 10-year equivalent — major advantage for batch preparation, long-term stockpiling, and field use
- RNA controls functional for both RT-PCR and RT-LAMP (point-of-care format)
- Room temperature shipping eliminates dry ice costs and regulatory complexities of air transport

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Preprint; not peer-reviewed as of submission date
- Accelerated aging validation uses Arrhenius model derived from earlier Fabre et al. work; model assumes constant degradation mechanism across temperature range
- Limited to synthetic RNA controls (not clinical patient samples)
- Validation focused on detection sensitivity; does not assess absolute quantification accuracy across laboratories
- Future: extension to other RNA pathogens, quantitative reference materials, clinical diagnostic standardization

## 7. Glossary
- **RNAshells**: Imagene's proprietary technology encapsulating vacuum-dried RNA in metallic capsules under anhydrous argon/helium atmosphere
- **Accelerated aging**: Heating at elevated temperature (e.g., 90°C) for defined time to simulate longer room-temperature storage; based on Arrhenius kinetics
- **Arrhenius model**: Mathematical model relating reaction rate to temperature; used to extrapolate RT storage lifetime from elevated-temperature degradation data
- **RT-LAMP**: Reverse Transcription Loop-Mediated Isothermal Amplification; point-of-care nucleic acid amplification without thermocycler
- **RT-qPCR**: Reverse Transcription quantitative PCR; gold-standard for RNA virus detection
- **RdRp**: RNA-dependent RNA polymerase; key SARS-CoV-2 gene target for molecular diagnostics
- **Sarbeco-E**: Pan-sarbecovirus E gene primer set; broad detection of SARS-related coronaviruses
