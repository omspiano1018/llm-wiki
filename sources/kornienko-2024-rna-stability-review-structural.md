## One-line Summary
Comprehensive review of internal and external factors governing RNA stability, degradation mechanisms, and approaches to RNA stabilization for biobanking and therapeutic applications.

## 2. Key Contributions
- Systematic review of external factors affecting RNA stability: temperature, pH, ionic composition, oxidative stress
- Review of internal structural factors: primary structure, secondary/tertiary structure, poly(A) tail length and composition, RNA–protein interactions
- Comparison of mRNA, rRNA, tRNA, and ncRNA stability profiles
- Formulation of evidence-based recommendations for RNA storage and biobanking
- Discussion of chemical modifications (e.g., m6A, pseudouridine) as stabilizing strategies

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Narrative literature review synthesizing decades of RNA stability research
- Covers chemical mechanisms: 2'-OH-mediated hydrolysis of phosphodiester bonds, transesterification, oxidation by ROS
- Discusses the role of divalent cations (Ca2+, Mg2+, transition metals) as hydrolysis catalysts
- Reviews poly(A) tail effects: short (<50 nt) tails susceptible to exonuclease; long (>150 nt) tails may impair ribosome binding
- Examines N6-methyladenosine (m6A) and hydroxymethylation effects on tail stability
- Phosphodiester bonds in RNA are 200× less stable than DNA (Lindahl et al.) due to 2'-OH
- At pH ~7.4, the 2'-OH nucleophilically attacks the adjacent phosphate, forming a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate intermediate

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Alkaline pH (e.g., 8.0) accelerates hydrolysis by increasing hydroxide ion availability
- Temperature elevation is a primary destabilizer; cold storage (−20°C, −80°C) is standard
- Oxidative stress (ROS, ozone) damages RNA bases and backbone; antioxidants can mitigate this
- RNA–protein interactions (e.g., poly(A)-binding proteins, RNA-binding proteins) protect specific structural regions
- Chemical modifications such as 2'-OMe, 2'-F, and pseudouridine (Ψ) substitutions improve stability and reduce immunogenicity
- Secondary structures (stem-loops, G-quadruplexes) provide local protection against nuclease access
- Recommendations: avoid metal ion contamination, maintain slightly acidic pH, store RNA dry or at ultra-low temperatures, use RNase inhibitors

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- Review does not present original experimental data; conclusions are based on cited literature
- Accelerated aging data from commercial vendors is often proprietary with limited independent validation
- Interplay between multiple stabilizing factors remains incompletely characterized
- Future work needed on novel stabilization matrices for biobanking (e.g., ionic liquids, deep eutectic solvents)
- mRNA vaccine stability under field conditions (humidity, temperature cycling) requires more study

## 6. Related Work
- Lindahl et al.: foundational comparison of DNA vs. RNA phosphodiester bond stability
- Foundational studies on RNAi, mRNA vaccines (Pfizer/BioNTech Comirnaty, Moderna Spikevax)
- Commercial stabilization approaches: Biomatrica RNAstable, Qiagen AllProtect
- Literature on N1-methylpseudouridine modification used in COVID-19 vaccines

## 7. Glossary
- **Phosphodiester bond**: Covalent bond linking nucleotides in the RNA backbone; hydrolyzed via 2'-OH attack
- **2',3'-cyclic phosphate**: Intermediate formed during transesterification; further hydrolyzed to 2'- or 3'-phosphate
- **m6A (N6-methyladenosine)**: RNA base modification affecting stability and protein binding
- **Poly(A) tail**: 3' adenosine repeat that protects mRNA from exonucleolytic degradation
- **RNase**: Ribonuclease enzyme that degrades RNA; major concern in sample handling
- **ROS**: Reactive oxygen species; oxidative agents that damage RNA bases and backbone
- **Pseudouridine (Ψ)**: Modified uridine used in therapeutic mRNA to reduce immunogenicity and improve stability
- **Biobank**: Repository of biological samples (including RNA) for research or diagnostic use
