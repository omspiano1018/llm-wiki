## One-line Summary
Comprehensive 2026 review of drying technologies for mRNA-LNPs and other nucleic acid-LNPs (siRNA, saRNA, circRNA, pDNA, miRNA, CRISPR), covering lyophilization, spray drying, and emerging alternatives, with attention to nucleic acid structure effects on drying behavior.

## 2. Key Contributions
- First review to systematically compare drying strategies across all major nucleic acid-LNP modalities: mRNA, siRNA, saRNA, circRNA, pDNA, miRNA, and CRISPR system
- Shows that nucleic acid structure (single-stranded vs. double-stranded, size, circular vs. linear) fundamentally affects drying challenges and optimal strategies
- Reviews not only conventional lyophilization but also: continuous freeze-drying (spin-freezing), thin film freeze-drying, spray freeze-drying, spray drying, and alcohol dilution freeze-drying
- Places mRNA-LNP freeze-drying in historical context of nucleic acid therapy development (first FDA nucleic acid drug: fomivirsen/Vitravene, 1998; siRNA Onpattro, 2018; COVID-19 mRNA vaccines, 2020)
- References 2023 Nobel Prize (Karikó and Weissman) for nucleoside modification enabling mRNA vaccines

## 3. Methodology and Architecture
- Comprehensive narrative review structured by: (1) mRNA-LNP structure and instability, (2) freeze-drying for mRNA-LNPs, (3) drying of other NA-LNPs, (4) alternative drying methods
- mRNA-LNPs contain ionizable lipid, helper lipid (DSPC surface localization, cholesterol concentration in core), PEG-lipid, and core water (~24% v/v, Yanez Arteta et al.)
- LNP core structure: disordered inverse hexagonal (Arteta et al.); core-shell with DSPC/cholesterol on surface, ionizable lipid in core (Sebastiani et al.); bleb structures (Brader et al. and multiple groups)
- mRNA instability: 2'-OH mediated hydrolysis (accelerated by alkaline conditions, temperature, humidity, ionic strength, dielectric constant, mRNA length); also oxidation, depurination, deamination
- Novel instability: mRNA-lipid adduct formation (Packer et al. 2021)
- Standard lyophilization: 3 steps (freezing, primary drying by sublimation, secondary drying by desorption)
- Alternative methods reviewed: spin-freezing continuous lyophilization, thin film freeze-drying, spray freeze-drying, spray drying, alcohol dilution freeze-drying

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks
- Spikevax: −15°C to −50°C storage; Comirnaty: −60°C to −80°C — standard lyophilization target is to eliminate these requirements
- Onpattro (siRNA-LNP): stable as liquid at 2-8°C for 3 years — benchmark for NA-LNP stability
- mRNA-LNP bleb structure: confirmed by multiple independent groups; influenced by buffer system, lipid composition, preparation methods, and lyophilization process
- RNA hydrolysis accelerated by: increased temperature, relative humidity, dielectric constant of solvent, low ionic strength, highly acidic/alkaline conditions, longer mRNA length
- 22 nucleic acid therapeutics approved by FDA/EMA (as of review date)
- Alcohol dilution freeze-drying: novel approach allowing freeze-drying of formulations that would otherwise be destabilized by aqueous freeze-concentration effects

## 5. Limitations and Future Work
- LNP structural heterogeneity (bleb formation) complicates optimization and standardization of freeze-drying cycles
- Alternative drying methods have not yet been validated for large-scale mRNA-LNP manufacturing
- Drying strategies developed for mRNA may not translate directly to other NA-LNPs (siRNA, saRNA, circRNA) due to structural differences
- Spray drying subjects formulations to heat and shear stress that can damage LNPs; balancing protective excipients with drying efficiency remains challenging
- Long-term storage stability of lyophilized NA-LNPs (beyond 1-2 years) requires further clinical-scale validation

## 6. Related Work
- Patisiran (Onpattro): first FDA-approved siRNA-LNP (2018); stable liquid at 2-8°C benchmark
- Comirnaty (BioNTech/Pfizer) and Spikevax (Moderna): COVID-19 mRNA vaccines; ultra-cold storage benchmarks
- Karikó and Weissman 2023 Nobel Prize: nucleoside modification of mRNA
- Yanez Arteta et al.: mRNA-LNP core structure with ~24% v/v water
- Sebastiani et al.: core-shell LNP structure
- Brader et al.: bleb structure in mRNA-LNPs
- Packer et al. (2021): mRNA-lipid adducts

## 7. Glossary
- **saRNA (self-amplifying RNA)**: RNA replicon that self-amplifies in cells, allowing lower dose mRNA vaccines
- **circRNA (circular RNA)**: Covalently closed circular RNA; resistant to exonuclease degradation
- **pDNA (plasmid DNA)**: Circular double-stranded DNA used in gene therapy
- **CRISPR system**: RNA-guided genome editing system; RNP or LNP delivery used for in vivo applications
- **Bleb structure**: Solvent-filled vesicular compartment in mRNA-LNPs; structurally distinct from standard liposome
- **Thin film freeze-drying**: LNP suspension spread as thin film before freezing; faster heat/mass transfer for uniform drying
- **Spray freeze-drying**: Atomized LNP droplets frozen by liquid nitrogen or cold gas before lyophilization
- **Spray drying**: Liquid LNP formulation atomized into hot drying gas; produces dry powder but subjects LNPs to heat and shear stress
- **Alcohol dilution freeze-drying**: Freeze-drying of LNP formulations prepared with ethanol dilution; mitigates freeze-concentration effects
